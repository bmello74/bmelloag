What gypsum does in Valley soils

Gypsum is calcium sulfate. It supplies calcium and sulfate sulfur without raising soil pH, which makes it the standard tool for the sodium and infiltration problems that come with Central Valley irrigation water.

  • Calcium replaces sodium on clay particles so sodium can be leached below the root zone.
  • Flocculated clay lets water move in instead of sealing, ponding or running off.
  • Better infiltration means deeper roots and less crusting at emergence.
  • Low-salt, low-sodium irrigation water gets the electrolyte it needs to penetrate.
  • Sulfate sulfur is plant-available right away for protein and oil production.

How much to apply depends on your soil, your water and your crop. A water analysis and saturated paste test answer that question with numbers.

Why TR-100 is 100% gypsum equivalent

TR-100 is a natural blend of about 60% dihydrate gypsum and 40% anhydrite, as registered with CDFA. Anhydrite is calcium sulfate without the bound water, so it is more concentrated: pure anhydrite works out to about 126% gypsum equivalent.

Blending the two puts TR-100 at a guaranteed 100% gypsum equivalent with 24% calcium and 19% sulfur. That means more calcium and sulfur on every truck and fewer tons to spread for the same result.
