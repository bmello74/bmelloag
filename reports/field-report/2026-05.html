Section 1

Crop Status & Growing Season

May puts the Central Valley into full gear — daytime highs climbing into the 80s and 90s, fruit and nut crops driving heavy biomass, and irrigation demand surging. Corn and cotton planting are active, wheat is heading into grain fill, and tree crops are pivoting from set into sizing. Pest pressure rises sharply this month — biological control programs deployed now pay off through July and August.

🍐  Almonds Nut Sizing
Hull and shell hardening underway. Kernel fill is the priority — deliver consistent water and K. Navel orangeworm 2nd flight monitoring critical as we head toward hull split. Mite pressure climbing fast in warm weather — scout twice weekly. Begin hull split planning now; first crackouts expected in July.
🌿  Pistachios Post-Bloom / Nut Fill
Bloom complete; nuts setting and shell expansion active. Resume aggressive irrigation and fertigation. 2026 on-year crop showing strong promise. Botryosphaeria, Alternaria late blight, and Gill’s mealybug all become threats this month. Begin scouting now — biological controls work best before populations explode.
🪓  Walnuts Pollination / Nut Set
Full leaf-out; pollination wrapping up; nutlets sizing. Codling moth 1A flight peaking — treatment timing critical based on degree-day model. Walnut blight risk persists if any late spring moisture. Watch for walnut husk fly degree days starting to accumulate. Aphids climbing — biologicals can knock back populations before bloom of secondaries.
🍇  Wine & Table Grapes Bloom / Cap Fall
Bloom and fruit set window — the make-or-break period for the season. Frost risk has passed. Maintain mildew program aggressively — bloom is highest powdery mildew vulnerability. Watch for thrips on flower clusters — biological options now well-supported by research. Coachella table grape harvest active; SJV in early shoot growth to bloom.
🥝  Kiwis Bloom Approach
Bloom is imminent — mid-to-late May for most varieties. Pollinator hives should already be in place or arriving this week. Final cane training and tying must be wrapped up. Botrytis pressure peaks during bloom with cool foggy nights. Watch for Bt moth and leafroller activity — biologicals fit cleanly into kiwi IPM.
🍊  Citrus Fruit Set / Sizing
Petal fall complete; June drop window approaching. Resume normal irrigation. Citrus thrips peak in May — thrips scarring window is now. Asian Citrus Psyllid (HLB vector) monitoring is a top priority — report suspicious symptoms to CDFA immediately. Watch for citrus leafminer and red scale.
🌿  Cotton Planting / Emergence
Planting active; early-planted fields emerging. Soil temps now reliably above 65°F. Push to complete planting by mid-May for full season potential. Thrips are the #1 stand-killer on emerging cotton — biological options shine here. Watch for early aphid colonization on cotyledons. Pre-emerge herbicide carryover should now be cleared.
🌽  Corn (Silage & Grain) Planting / V2–V6
Main planting window in full swing through mid-May. Earliest-planted silage corn already at V4–V6. First side-dress N at V6–V8 — the largest yield-determining nutrient call of the season. Watch for cutworm and corn leafminer. Spider mites can emerge fast in hot, dry conditions. Biological N-fixers shine on early-planted fields.
🌾  Wheat (Winter) Heading / Grain Fill
Heads emerging to early grain fill. Last fertility window has effectively closed. Aphid populations spike in May warmth — check for honeydew and BYDV symptoms. Stripe rust still a threat through milky stage. Begin hardening off — reduce final irrigation to allow for proper grain dry-down ahead of June/early July harvest.

Section 2

May Fertilizer Requirements

May is when nutrient demand truly accelerates. Tree crops are sizing fruit and nuts; wheat is finishing; cotton needs precision-timed starter and side-dress N. Fertigation is the dominant delivery method now — matching uptake curves with split applications is critical. Pair fertility programs with biological inputs for compounding returns.

Crop Primary Nutrients Micros / Notes Timing & Method
Almonds N: 30–50 lbs/ac via fertigation; peak K demand for hull/kernel sizing. Continue split applications. Foliar Zn & B if May tissue test indicates. HIGH Biological N fixers help reduce synthetic N load. Fertigation every 7–10 days through hull split. Tissue test mid-May to fine-tune.
Pistachios Resume aggressive program post-bloom. N: 40–60 lbs/ac in May. K demand high for nut fill. Zn corrective foliar if needed. HIGH B for shell integrity. Fertigation 2× per week ideal. Begin Ca delivery for shell.
Walnuts N: deliver 30% of season total in May. K critical for nut fill. Continue split fertigation. Zn foliar if needed. HIGH Mn corrections via foliar. Fertigation via micro-sprinklers. Tissue sample late May for July adjustments.
Grapes Hold heavy N during bloom. K demand peaking; deliver via fertigation through fruit set. B at pre-bloom essential. HIGH Mg foliar if mid-leaf yellowing. Fertigation. No foliar during open bloom — before or after only.
Kiwis Continue 40 lbs/ac N May into June. Hold heavy N during bloom. K via fertigation. B & Ca foliar pre-bloom. HIGH Mg if pale leaves. Fertigation every 10–14 days. Foliars before bloom only.
Citrus Resume normal N: 25–30 lbs/ac in May. K critical for fruit sizing through June drop. Zn & Mn foliar. MED Iron chelate if young leaves chlorotic. Fertigation every 7–14 days. Pair with biological inoculants.
Cotton Starter N at emergence: 15–20 lbs/ac. First side-dress at 4–6 leaf stage: 30–40 lbs N/ac. Zn pre-plant or in-furrow. MED Biological N fixers boost early-season vigor. In-furrow starter; first side-dress before squaring; consider biological inoculation.
Corn Starter N at planting: 20–30 lbs/ac. First side-dress at V6–V8: 80–120 lbs N/ac — biggest yield call of the season. P & K based on soil test. Zn in-furrow on alkaline soils. HIGH S: 15–25 lbs/ac on sandy or low-OM soils. Biological N-fixers stretch synthetic N. In-furrow starter at planting. Side-dress UAN/urea V6–V8. Fertigation through tassel.
Wheat No additional N or P this month — window has closed. Focus on grain fill management via irrigation. No foliar micros at heading. LOW Stick with prior plan. Begin reducing irrigation to harden grain. Last irrigation typically late May.
Advisor Note: May tissue tests should drive June rate decisions. Heat stress during fertigation events reduces uptake efficiency by 30–50%. Run fertigations early morning or late evening when possible. Couple fertility programs with biological inoculants — healthy soil biology amplifies fertilizer efficiency and reduces synthetic input requirements.

This Month in American History

May 8, 1945 — V-E Day: Victory in Europe

On May 8, 1945, the guns finally fell silent across Europe. After nearly six years of war, Nazi Germany formally surrendered to the Allied forces, ending the deadliest conflict the European continent had ever seen. President Harry S. Truman declared the day Victory in Europe Day — V-E Day — and dedicated the victory to the memory of his predecessor, Franklin D. Roosevelt, who had died just weeks earlier without seeing the triumph he helped lead.

From Times Square to small farming towns across the Central Valley, Americans poured into the streets to celebrate. Church bells rang. Families wept for sons, brothers, and fathers who would never come home. More than 400,000 Americans had given their lives in the war — on the beaches of Normandy, in the forests of the Ardennes, in the skies over Germany, and across the Pacific. The harvest of 1945 would be brought in by men and women who had stood in freedom’s defense.

V-E Day stands as a permanent reminder of what a free people can accomplish when they stand together. American farmers fed the war effort. American factories built the arsenal of democracy. And American servicemen and women carried the cost across two oceans to liberate a continent. Eighty-one years later, the freedom they secured is the same freedom under which we plant, grow, and harvest today.

“

The mission of this Allied Force was fulfilled at 0241, local time, May 7, 1945.

— General Dwight D. Eisenhower
Cable to the Combined Chiefs of Staff, May 7, 1945
