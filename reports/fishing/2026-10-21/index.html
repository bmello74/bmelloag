Salmon is open, with 11 days left on the calendar. From Pigeon Point up to Point Reyes the season runs through Oct. 31, which is 11 days counting today. The stretch south of Pigeon Point has been closed since Oct. 1. CDFW’s salmon page and its Marine Management News blog, both read this morning, show no change to the fall season; the newest salmon action on the blog is still Aug. 21. CDFW’s catch tracker, read directly, still runs through Sept. 30: 11,040 caught, 8,960 left on the 20,000-fish guideline, and the Oct. 1–15 estimate hasn’t posted. At September’s pace of about 230 fish a day, all 31 days of October would take roughly 7,100 fish, short of the 8,960 left. That is our arithmetic, not a CDFW forecast, but it says the calendar, not the guideline, is the likelier thing to end this season.

The kings are thinning. Friday through Tuesday the boats the count service listed landed 231 kings on 232 rods, about 1.00 a rod, against 1.57 a week ago. Friday ran 1.17, Saturday 0.90, Sunday 0.74, and no salmon boats were listed Monday. The standouts: Oakland Anglers II with 26 on 17 Friday, Lovely Martha with 23 on 22, fish to 27 pounds, Sunday, and Hog Heaven with 16 kings to 28 pounds Friday. Tuesday is one boat: New Easy Rider, 14 on 7 rods, a limit for everyone aboard. Fish Emeryville’s Tuesday post said “Salmon is still on the menu!!” The count service may not list every boat.

Bluefin turned up in Monterey Bay, and the south went to yellowtail. New Horizon, a Monterey half-day boat, listed 16 bluefin on nine rods Saturday morning. At Newport, Western Pride had 185 yellowtail on 44 rods Sunday. San Diego and Oceanside boats listed 3,207 yellowfin and 24 bluefin Friday through Tuesday, and two 3-day boats came in Monday with bluefin to 200 pounds. The statewide sport mussel quarantine ran through at least Oct. 31 as of the Oct. 12 notice; we did not re-read CDPH this week.

The full moon brings minus tides back. It is full Sunday, Oct. 25, at about 9:11 p.m. The Golden Gate low goes below zero Sunday at 5:34 p.m. (−0.34 ft) and reaches −1.10 ft at 7:52 p.m. next Wednesday. Pine Flat is up 6,300 acre-feet on the week, to 17.8% full, the only reservoir of the group that gained. And CDFW’s trout plants have reached the foothills: this week’s list shows McClure, the Merced River, Modesto and Woodward Reservoirs, Yosemite Lake, Shaver and Bass Lake.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat is climbing faster again. The Corps sheet (data through Tuesday midnight) has the lake at 177,670 AF, 17.8% full, up 6,300 AF since last Tuesday and up 1,180 on Tuesday alone, against last week’s gain of 394 on the day. The release was 182 to 192 cfs for most of the last 48 hours, then stepped up to 216 cfs by 6 a.m. today; it was about 250 cfs a week ago. The sheet’s computed inflow is 792 cfs, while the gauge on the Kings below the North Fork, at the head of the lake, reads 193. We still can’t reconcile the two, and the Corps itself warns its computed inflow is erratic. The storage gain comes from the elevation gauge.

The south Valley lakes keep sliding. Isabella lost 3,080 AF on the week, to 24.6%, releasing 298 cfs against 211 coming in. Success lost 224 and is now 9.6%. Kaweah lost 67, to 10.3%. Eastman is 6.7%. Hensley reported nothing on today’s sheet; its last reading was 7.4% on Oct. 6.

The foothill lakes all dropped. The Corps’ Section 7 sheet carries nothing for New Melones, Don Pedro, McClure or Shaver this week, only Millerton and Bass Lake, so we went to the state’s California Data Exchange Center (CDEC). Through Sunday, Oct. 18: Don Pedro 73.7%, down 14,415 AF in six days; McClure 50.7%, down 8,954. Through Monday, Oct. 19: New Melones 62.8%, down 10,373 in six days, and Shaver 52.7%, down 1,146 in seven. Millerton is 43.6%, down 2,437. Pine Flat is the only one that gained. Oro Madre Bass Anglers has Don Pedro Saturday and the Delta is crowded with events Saturday and Sunday. Expect trailers.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

If you’re planning an island trip, ask whoever you book with where their boats have been fishing.

Rockfish rules changed Oct. 1 for every SoCal boat, island trips included. From Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, south of Point Conception, nearshore rockfish, cabezon and greenling are closed to boat anglers, and shelf rockfish and lingcod may only be taken seaward of the 50-fathom line. Shore anglers and divers are exempt. Yellowtail, calico, bonito and tuna aren’t affected. We re-read CDFW’s groundfish summary this morning for that. Also in force as of CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice, which we did not re-read this week: yellowfin 10 a day within the 20-fish general limit, bluefin 2 on top of it, and the emergency rule from Pigeon Point south: no hooks over 1½ inches and no wire leaders for shore anglers or anyone within 1,000 yards of shore.

Two salmon a day, any species except coho, 20-inch minimum. No filleting on the boat. North of Point Conception: two single-point, single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler.

Call the hotline the morning you go: (707) 576-3429. Inseason closures can come with only a few days’ notice. That’s what happened to San Francisco in August.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish, cabezon and greenling per day, aggregate. Within that: copper 1, canary 2, and vermilion/sunset 2. The vermilion limit is 4 only in the Northern Management Area, north of Cape Mendocino, and 2 everywhere else (re-checked this morning against CDFW’s groundfish summary). Lingcod 2 a day, 22-inch minimum. No retention at any time of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted.

Depths: Northern, Mendocino, San Francisco and Central areas (everything north of Point Conception) are open at all depths through Dec. 31. Southern area: nearshore species closed; shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of 50 fathoms only from Oct. 1. You must carry a descending device. Cordell Bank: all groundfish fishing is prohibited there, per CDFW. Check the boundary on CDFW’s Ocean Sport Fishing interactive map and the NMFS compliance guide before you go.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Minus tides mean fast water at dusk. From Sunday the Golden Gate drains 6 to 8 feet in under seven hours, and the flood comes back just as fast over rocks, jetties and bars you walked out to on the ebb. If you’re on the rocks, a flat or a sandbar after dark, know when the tide turns, carry a real light, and give yourself a way back before it does.

The Kings release stepped up overnight. The Corps’ hourly report has the flow below Pine Flat at 216 cfs by 6 a.m. today, after two days near 190. It’s still cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can change again. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

Shellfish: we did not re-read CDPH this week. As of the Oct. 12 notice the Monterey County warning was lifted, and the statewide sport mussel quarantine ran through at least Oct. 31, so no mussels anywhere until you’ve checked. We also saw no notice lifting CDFW’s Sept. 11 order making anchovy taken from Pigeon Point to Point Lobos bait only, so treat that as still in force. Shellfish line: (800) 553-4133.

Young white sharks may still be close to shore. CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice set an emergency rule from Pigeon Point south that bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. We didn’t re-read the notice this week, so check CDFW before you fish that stretch. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 17.8%, Success 9.6%, Kaweah 10.3%, Eastman 6.7%, and Don Pedro, McClure, New Melones and Shaver all lost thousands of acre-feet. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is closer to prop depth now, and the Delta and Don Pedro will be crowded Saturday. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind and fire season aren’t over. Check road, fire and wind before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, salmon. Pigeon Point to Point Reyes is open through Oct. 31, 11 days counting today. CDFW’s tracker still runs only through Sept. 30: 11,040 caught and 8,960 left on the 20,000-fish guideline, so the calendar, not the count, will probably end the season. But the Bay boats are thinning, with 231 kings on 232 rods since Friday, about 1.00 a rod, down from 1.57. Call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, the full moon and the minus tides. It’s full Sunday night, Oct. 25, and the Golden Gate goes below zero every evening from Sunday, to −1.10 ft next Wednesday. Mind the water coming back over the rocks.

Third, the lakes. Pine Flat is up 6,300 AF, to 17.8% full, and it’s the only one of the group that gained. The south Valley lakes and the foothill lakes kept dropping. The good news is trout: CDFW’s plant list reaches McClure, Modesto and Woodward Reservoirs, Yosemite Lake, Shaver and Bass Lake this week. We also couldn’t get a usable freshwater bite report beyond Bridgeport, so the bite table says so. Call the marina before you tow.
