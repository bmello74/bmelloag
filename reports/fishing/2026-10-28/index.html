Salmon is open, with four days left on the calendar. From Pigeon Point up to Point Reyes the season runs through Saturday, Oct. 31, which is four days counting today. The stretch south of Pigeon Point has been closed since Oct. 1. CDFW’s salmon page and its Marine Management News blog, both read this morning, show no change to the fall season; the newest salmon action on the blog is still Aug. 21. CDFW’s catch tracker, read directly, now runs through Oct. 15: 13,769 caught, 6,231 left on the 20,000-fish guideline. That is 2,729 fish since the Sept. 30 reading, about 180 a day. Another 16 days at that pace is roughly 2,900, well short of 6,231. That is our arithmetic, not a CDFW forecast, but it says the calendar, not the guideline, is what ends this season.

The kings are holding at a steady, modest clip. Friday through Monday the boats the count service listed landed 161 kings on 173 rods, about 0.93 a rod, against 1.00 a week ago. Friday ran 0.75, Saturday 1.00, Sunday 0.91 and Monday 1.20 on two boats. The standouts: Lovely Martha with 24 on 21 Sunday, Outer Limits and Argo with 10 on 5 each, limits, Saturday, and New Easy Rider with 8 on 4, limits, Monday, after 14 on 14 (fish to 26 pounds) Sunday. Tuesday had no scores posted. Fish Emeryville’s own post says this is its last week for salmon, with trips through Saturday the 31st. The count service may not list every boat.

The lingcod and rockfish are limiting out, and San Diego is on yellowfin. California Dawn II (Berkeley) listed 40 lingcod to 22 pounds and 200 rockfish on 20 rods Saturday, New Huck Finn (Emeryville) 48 lingcod and 240 rockfish on 24 Monday, and Morro Bay’s Endeavor 20 lingcod on 10 rods Sunday, limits both ways. In San Diego, Pacific Queen landed 251 yellowfin to 90 pounds on 22 rods on a 3-day trip Sunday, and New Lo-An had 118 yellowfin to 65 pounds on 24 rods on a 2-day Monday. Newport’s yellowtail cooled off to bonito, and Monterey Bay’s bluefin all but vanished from this week’s counts.

Tonight’s tide is the one to know. The Golden Gate falls from a 6.67 ft high at 12:56 p.m. to −1.10 ft at 7:52 p.m., the lowest water of our window, and stays below zero in the evening through Sunday night. Clocks fall back at 2 a.m. Sunday, Nov. 1, so the minus lows start landing an hour earlier on the clock. Pine Flat is up again, +3,542 acre-feet on the week to 18.1%, the only major lake that gained. And a note for planning: the permit database lists 17 bass events starting Saturday, Nov. 7 and Fish Emeryville says crab season opens that day (our note: that is the landing’s claim, not CDFW’s).

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat is up again, but more slowly. The Corps sheet (data through Tuesday midnight, generated 7 a.m. today) has the lake at 181,212 AF, 18.1% full, up 3,542 AF since last Tuesday against last week’s gain of 6,300, and up 239 on Tuesday alone. The Corps’ hourly report has the release steady at 201 cfs for the last 48 hours, against 216 at last week’s reading. The Kings below the North Fork, at the head of the lake, reads 240 to 250 cfs, and the sheet’s computed inflow is 331 cfs; the two still don’t reconcile, and the Corps itself warns its computed inflow is erratic. The storage gain comes from the elevation gauge.

A small pulse came through the south Valley rivers. Kaweah at Three Rivers (CDEC daily mean) went from 44 cfs on Oct. 21 to 127 cfs on Oct. 23 and was back to 68 on Tuesday; Merced near Snelling ran 912 cfs on Oct. 23 and 275 on Tuesday. Kaweah gained 639 AF to 10.6%, Success 124 to 9.7% and Eastman 120 to 6.8%. Isabella still lost 684 AF, to 24.5%, releasing 269 cfs against 227 coming in. Hensley is back on the sheet at 7.4%, 6,622 AF.

The foothill lakes are still dropping. Millerton is 42.5%, down 5,752 AF on the week. From the state’s California Data Exchange Center (CDEC): Don Pedro 72.7% through Sunday, down 21,425 AF in eight days, and McClure 49.5% through Sunday, down 12,055 in seven. Shaver is 51.0%, down 2,356 in seven days. New Melones, on the Corps’ sheet today, is 62.8%, down only 1,769 in eight days; it has nearly leveled off. Pine Flat is the only one that gained. Expect crowded ramps Sunday: the permit database has events on both McClure and Don Pedro Nov. 1.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish, cabezon and greenling per day, aggregate. Within that: copper 1, canary 2, and vermilion/sunset 2. The vermilion limit is 4 only in the Northern Management Area, north of Cape Mendocino, and 2 everywhere else (re-checked this morning against CDFW’s groundfish summary). Lingcod 2 a day, 22-inch minimum. No retention at any time of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted.

Depths: Northern, Mendocino, San Francisco and Central areas (everything north of Point Conception) are open at all depths through Dec. 31. Southern area: nearshore species closed; shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of 50 fathoms only from Oct. 1. You must carry a descending device. Cordell Bank: all groundfish fishing is prohibited there, per CDFW. Check the boundary on CDFW’s Ocean Sport Fishing interactive map and the NMFS compliance guide before you go.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

Local time: daylight time through Saturday, standard time from Sunday. Solunar majors are the moon overhead and underfoot, in the order they fall in the day. ⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Minus tides mean fast water at dusk. Tonight the Golden Gate drains 7.77 feet in under seven hours, to −1.10 ft at 7:52 p.m., and tomorrow to −0.98 ft at 8:47 p.m. The flood comes back just as fast over rocks, jetties and bars you walked out to on the ebb. If you’re on the rocks, a flat or a sandbar after dark, know when the tide turns, carry a real light, and give yourself a way back before it does.

Clocks go back Sunday, and dark comes an hour earlier. Sunset drops from 6:03 p.m. Saturday to 5:02 p.m. Sunday. Plan your launch and your drive home around the new light.

The Kings release is steady, and still cold. The Corps’ hourly report had the flow below Pine Flat at 196 to 201 cfs through the last 48 hours. It’s cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can change overnight. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

Shellfish: we did not re-read CDPH this week. As of the Oct. 12 notice the Monterey County warning was lifted, and the statewide sport mussel quarantine ran through at least Oct. 31, which is Saturday, so check CDPH for an extension before you gather a single mussel. We also saw no notice lifting CDFW’s Sept. 11 order making anchovy taken from Pigeon Point to Point Lobos bait only, so treat that as still in force. Shellfish line: (800) 553-4133.

Young white sharks may still be close to shore. CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice set an emergency rule from Pigeon Point south that bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. We didn’t re-read the notice this week, so check CDFW before you fish that stretch. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 18.1%, Success 9.7%, Kaweah 10.6%, Eastman 6.8%, Hensley 7.4%, and Don Pedro, McClure, New Melones, Millerton and Shaver all lost thousands of acre-feet. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is closer to prop depth now. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind and fire season aren’t over. Check road, fire and wind before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, salmon. Pigeon Point to Point Reyes is open through Saturday, Oct. 31, four days counting today. CDFW’s tracker runs through Oct. 15: 13,769 caught and 6,231 left on the 20,000-fish guideline, so the calendar, not the count, will probably end the season. The Bay boats listed 161 kings on 173 rods Friday through Monday, about 0.93 a rod, with Fish Emeryville calling this its last week for salmon. Call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, the tide and the clock. The Golden Gate goes to −1.10 ft tonight and fades to above zero by Tuesday, and the clocks go back Sunday. Mind the water coming back over the rocks, and mind the early dark.

Third, the lakes. Pine Flat is up 3,542 AF, to 18.1% full, and the small southern lakes inched up with it. Don Pedro, McClure, Millerton and Shaver all kept dropping. This week’s trout plant list is Don Pedro, New Melones and the Kings below Pine Flat. We also couldn’t get a usable freshwater bite report, so the bite table says so. Call the marina before you tow.
