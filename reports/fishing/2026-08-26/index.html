Correction first, because it matters more than anything else in this issue. Last week we told you San Francisco and Monterey both ran through August 31 — thirteen days. On August 19 the National Marine Fisheries Service took inseason action and cut San Francisco off after Sunday the 23rd. The area is closed as of Monday. CDFW’s numbers say 26,984 of the 34,900-Chinook summer guideline were already in the box through August 15, and the catch rate was going to eat the rest. So if you were holding a Saturday reservation out of Berkeley, Emeryville or Sausalito for a king, that trip is gone.

Monterey and south is still open, and it has six days. Today through Monday the 31st, or until the 21,800-fish guideline fills, whichever lands first. Then the fall season opens Tuesday, September 1 — inside this coverage window — from 38°02′N near Point Reyes down to Pigeon Point through October 31, and Pigeon Point south through September 30. Both areas share one 20,000-fish guideline, which is a smaller number than it sounds like.

The bottom, meanwhile, is having the best week we have printed. Sunday there were seventeen rockfish trips posted between Bodega and Monterey. Sixteen came in at exactly ten a rod. The seventeenth was one fish short of it. Four boats doubled up with full lingcod limits on top. If you cannot get a salmon, you can absolutely get a sack.

Inland, one piece of real news and one piece of missing paperwork. CDFW is putting rainbows in the Kings River below Pine Flat Dam this week — the first plant on our valley-adjacent list all summer. And the Corps’ Section 7 sheet quietly dropped Don Pedro, McClure, Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence and every Friant canal gage this cycle. We are not going to reprint nine-day-old numbers as if they were current. Details in Water Watch.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat: another 6.4 feet gone. From 760.47 ft and 202,375 acre-feet a week ago to 754.08 ft and 186,532 acre-feet at midnight Tuesday. That is 15,843 acre-feet in seven days and it puts the lake at 18.7% of gross pool — under twenty for the first time this season. Two weeks ago it was 20.2%. Three weeks ago the ramps you are used to still worked.

The release came back down. Pine Flat is putting out 1,884 cfs, down from 2,174 last week. Over five weeks that line reads 4,226, 3,071, 2,003, 2,174, 1,884. It is trending down again, but we said last week not to take a trend line as a promise, and we are saying it again. Still not wadeable.

The upper Kings fell off a cliff. Kings natural flow into Pine Flat is 185.5 cfs against 422.0 last week — down 56% in seven days, and down from 587 two weeks ago. Below the North Fork near Trimmer is 236.4. The Kern at Kernville is 176.7, off from 207.6, the second straight drop. Kernville is still the friendliest wading water on our list, but it is shrinking fast and the window is closing.

The Section 7 sheet fell apart this cycle. Don Pedro, New Exchequer (McClure), Camanche, Del Valle, Los Banos and Big Dry Creek all came back “No Project Data Available at this Time.” Shaver, Huntington, Florence, Edison, Mammoth Pool and Redinger are all “-NR-”. So are the Friant-Kern Canal, the Madera Canal, the San Joaquin below Friant, flow below McSwain and the Tuolumne natural flow. Last week those figures were nine days old; this week they are simply gone. We have left those rows blank rather than reprint August 17 numbers with a straight face.

Two things did come back. Both Tule gages are reporting again after three blank weeks — South Fork near Success at 3.3 cfs, near Springville at 9.5 — and Kaweah at McKay Point is back at 14.0 cfs. Small numbers, but they are numbers. Goodwin release jumped to 1,388 cfs from 225 last week, which is a big move on the lower Stanislaus and worth knowing before you launch.

⚠ Before You Drive Over — Different Rulebook

Special regulations are the norm over there, not the exception. Individual Eastern Sierra waters carry their own gear restrictions, size limits, reduced bag limits and seasonal closures. Several run barbless-only or catch-and-release stretches, and Crowley in particular fishes under tighter August rules than the statewide standard — restricted baits and a reduced limit with a size minimum. Look up the specific water in the current CDFW freshwater regulations before you launch. Do not assume the five-fish valley rulebook travels with you.

Getting there from the valley: Tioga Pass (Highway 120) is the direct route to the June Lake Loop and it is a seasonal road — open now, but check Caltrans before an early start. Figure roughly five hours from Fresno to June Lake, six to Bridgeport. Sonora Pass (108) and Monitor Pass (89) are the northern alternatives.

Labor Day weekend is next weekend. Bridgeport, Twin Lakes and the June Loop fill up, and the fresh plants will draw a crowd. If you want water to yourself, go this week instead of next.

◉ Delta, Rivers & Aqueduct

The Delta — Fish the Tide, Because That Is What We Can Measure

Honest accounting on this section. The fall striped bass run we led with last week is still on — nothing has happened to reverse it, and it usually builds through September. But no new guide report was filed inside this window in the feeds we track, and the regional column that carries the named quotes publishes tomorrow, after we send. So we are not going to reprint last week’s Liberty Island and Frank’s Tract quotes with fresh dates on them. What follows is the standing pattern plus this week’s measured tide.

The tide is the actionable part, and it is good. Port Chicago runs a strong front half: today brings a 4.9 ft high at 12:44 a.m. against a 0.0 ft low at 8:22 a.m., and Thursday holds nearly the same shape. Then the lows climb every day — 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 — and by Tuesday the daily swing is a full foot smaller. Port Chicago never goes minus in this window; 0.0 ft is as low as it gets, this morning and tomorrow. The general rule stands: two hours before the high into the peak, blood worms, pile worms or fresh shad, with the better fish on shad.

For largemouth, the late-August pattern is wood and moving water — flip and punch the tule edges where there is current, big profiles on the high tide, and reaction baits when it blows. Small bass dominate the numbers. If the wind lays flat, prop baits with long pauses.

Sturgeon status, unchanged and still not law. The Fish and Game Commission voted 3–2 on August 15 to allow catch-and-release only for white sturgeon, with no harvest. As of this morning we have seen nothing confirming the Office of Administrative Law has acted, and no opening date has been published. Do not put a card in your pocket on the strength of this paragraph. Confirm with CDFW that the regulation is actually in effect before you fish.

California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay

San Luis and O’Neill are Reclamation and DWR facilities and appear on neither Corps sheet, so we still have no independently measured storage here — that has been true every week we have published. The standing pattern: bank stripers around Dinosaur Point on jumbo minnows, anchovies or blood worms; the forebay slowed by thick grass limiting access; trollers grinding between wind windows for a handful of fish and the occasional very large one.

Two things to know before you go. Boat inspections are required on exit at San Luis, O’Neill Forebay and Los Banos Creek — golden mussels were detected at the forebay, and boats leaving will not get a clean-boat tag. And in the south aqueduct in Kern County, the cooler water has kept striper action honest on cut sardines, anchovies or jumbo minnows, with catfish steady on dip bait in the calm water below check gates.

The state publishes a map of the 16 designated aqueduct fishing locations through the Department of Water Resources recreation pages. Worth having on your phone — access is not legal everywhere along that ditch.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety — Late August

Pine Flat is under twenty percent and has dropped sixteen and a half feet in fifteen days. That is the most dangerous number in this issue. Rock, stump and old roadbed that were well under your hull three weeks ago are at prop depth now, and the ramps are ending in dirt. Idle in, watch the sounder, and throw out every waypoint you set before August.

Goodwin release jumped from 225 to 1,388 cfs. That is a six-fold increase on the lower Stanislaus in one week and it is exactly the kind of change that catches waders and bank anglers out. Check it before you step in.

The lower Kings is at 1,884 cfs and getting planted this week. Fresh trout will pull people to the bank. It is still not a wading flow. Bank or boat below the dam, and wear the vest.

Fire danger is extreme in the high country. That is the ranger district’s wording, not ours. If you are headed to Edison, Florence, Mammoth Pool, Wishon, Courtright or Portal Forebay, check road and fire status before you leave and know your second way out.

Eastern Sierra? Different risks. Afternoon thunderstorms build fast over the crest, there is no cell coverage in the canyons, and altitude is real above 9,000 feet. Tell somebody your route and be off exposed water when the clouds stack up.

Heat. Valley afternoons are still the real risk on this list. Off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening. Water, shade, a hat, and somebody who knows where you went.

Two things to act on, and both of them are time-sensitive in a way this newsletter usually is not. If you want a king off a party boat this summer, you have six days and you have to launch south of Pigeon Point. San Francisco is closed and it closed a week ahead of the schedule we printed last Wednesday. Call the hotline before you drive.

And if you would rather fill a sack than chase one fish, go this week rather than next. The rockfish bite is at full limits on essentially every boat from Bodega to Avila, and the moon and tide both peak in the first three days of this window instead of the last three. After Thursday morning it flattens out. We do not usually say “go now” — this week we are saying it.
