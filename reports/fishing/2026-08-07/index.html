Two things line up this week and they line up well. The new moon arrives the night of August 12–13, and the biggest minus tides of the month land right on top of it — a run of negative lows from Saturday the 8th through Thursday the 13th, bottoming at −1.1 ft in the Bay and −1.2 ft at Monterey and Port San Luis. That is a lot of water moving, and moving water is most of the fight.

Offshore, the salmon are the story and have been for a week. Freshwater is the other side of the coin: the valley-floor reservoirs are getting drawn hard for irrigation, and a few of them are getting thin enough that where you launch matters more than what you throw.

◉ This Week’s Best Bets

 

● No. 1 — Ocean

SF Bay & Farallones King Salmon

Boats are scoring limits. Aug 5: Lovely Martha 36 kings for 18 anglers, California Dawn II 32 for 16. Aug 4 saw a fish to 32 pounds. Emeryville ran eight boats on salmon Wednesday and all of them found fish. Area open through Aug 31.

● No. 2 — Tides

Minus Tides, Mon–Wed

−0.9 ft Monday, −1.1 ft Tuesday and Wednesday at the Gate; −1.2 ft Monterey and Port San Luis Tuesday. Strong outgoing for Delta stripers, Bay halibut drifts, and the best clamming tides of August. All in the early morning.

● No. 3 — Central Coast

Rockfish, Near Limits Everywhere

Morro Bay’s Starfire put 230 rockfish on 25 rods; Endeavor 219 plus 7 lingcod on 23. Half Moon Bay’s Queen of Hearts hit a clean 270 for 27. Monterey’s Kahuna limited 11 anglers on a half day. Short trips, full sacks.

● No. 4 — Moon

New Moon Window, Aug 10–14

New moon falls the night of Aug 12–13. The almanac window brackets it: Aug 10 through Aug 14 rate best, with Aug 11–13 the peak. Dark nights, big tides, and majors sitting near first light. If you only get one trip, take one of those.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat is being pulled hard. It dropped 6,451 acre-feet in a single day, releasing 4,226 cfs against only 1,003 cfs of inflow. That puts the lake at roughly 26% of gross pool and it will keep sliding through the irrigation season. Ramp discipline: check where the water is before you trailer up.

That same 4,226 cfs is going down the lower Kings. It is high, cold, and pushy. Wading below the dam is a bad idea at this release — fish it from the bank or from a boat, and treat any rock you cannot see as one you do not want to stand on.

The small ones are getting small. Eastman is at 7% of gross pool and Hensley at 9%. Kaweah is at 16%. Launch ramps at these three are the thing to verify by phone before you drive, not after.

◉ Delta, Rivers & Aqueduct

The Delta

The tide book is doing the work this week. Port Chicago runs negative lows Monday through Thursday — −0.1 ft Monday, −0.2 Tuesday, −0.3 Wednesday, −0.2 Thursday, all mid-morning. Those are strong outgoing swings for a neap-to-spring transition, and stripers set up on the current edges when the water moves like that.

The tournament calendar tells you the west Delta is worth fishing: fourteen permitted events run in the Contra Costa, Sacramento and San Joaquin County reaches between Aug 7 and Aug 16. Nobody books fourteen tournaments on dead water.

No guide report was filed for the Delta this week. The tide and event data above are measured and verified; the fish are your problem.

California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay

No current storage or pumping figures for San Luis or O’Neill were available through the USACE reports this week — these are Reclamation and DWR facilities and do not appear on either the Corps or Section 7 sheet. August is when the forebay striper bite is driven almost entirely by pumping schedule: when water is moving, fish the boils and the current seams; when it stops, so does the bite. Check DWR before you drive out.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety — August in the Valley

The lower Kings is running 4,226 cfs. That is not a wading flow. It is cold, fast, and it will take your feet out from under you before you have decided anything. Fish it from the bank or from a boat, and wear the vest.

Reservoir drawdowns move hazards. Pine Flat is dropping 6,451 acre-feet a day and Isabella 2,429. Stumps, rock and old roadbed that were ten feet down last month are at prop depth now. Idle in, watch your sounder, and do not trust waypoints from July.

Heat. Valley afternoons are the real risk on this list. Be off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening. Water, shade, a hat, and somebody who knows where you went. Cold water and 100-degree air is how people get in trouble.

If you fish one day this week, make it Tuesday or Wednesday. New moon, the deepest minus tides of the month, and a salmon fleet that is putting two fish per rod on the deck — that combination shows up a handful of times a season.

And if you are heading up to Pine Flat or Kaweah, call the ramp first. The water is coming down faster than the signage is going up.
