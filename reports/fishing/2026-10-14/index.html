Salmon is open, with 18 days left on the calendar. From Pigeon Point up to Point Reyes the season runs through Oct. 31, which is 18 days counting today. The stretch south of Pigeon Point has been closed since Oct. 1. CDFW’s in-season changes page and its Marine Management News blog still show Aug. 21 as the newest salmon action, with no change to the fall season. CDFW’s catch tracker, which we read directly this morning, now runs through Sept. 30: 11,040 caught, 8,960 left on the 20,000-fish guideline, so 55.2% is gone. The Sept. 16–30 stretch added 3,455 fish, about 230 a day by our arithmetic. At that pace the 8,960 would outlast the 18 days on the calendar, but that is our math on one fortnight, not a CDFW forecast, and the Oct. 1–15 estimate hasn’t posted yet.

The Bay boats are still finding kings. Friday through Monday the boats the count service listed landed 326 kings on 208 rods, about 1.57 a rod. California Dawn II posted 24, 24, 23 and 25 kings on four straight days, with fish to 35 pounds Sunday. Tuesday is a thin day to judge: only New Easy Rider was listed, with 18 kings on 19 rods, to 24 pounds. Those are the boats the count service listed. It may not list them all.

Bluefin cooled off. Sorta Salty out of Bodega Bay listed 12 bluefin to 45 pounds on six rods Friday. San Diego and Oceanside boats listed 13 bluefin over four days, against the 145 we summed a week ago, and yellowfin is carrying the south. And the Monterey County shellfish warning was lifted Monday, Oct. 12. The statewide sport mussel quarantine still runs through at least Oct. 31.

No minus tides this week, and the moon is in its weak quarter. The lowest low at any of our four stations is 0.11 ft at the Golden Gate tonight at 8:45 p.m. The moon reaches first quarter at about 9:12 a.m. Sunday, Oct. 18, the almanac’s slowest stretch, with the full moon Oct. 25. Pine Flat is up 7,247 acre-feet on the week, to 17.1% full, but the gain has slowed to about 400 acre-feet a day from last week’s 1,300.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat is still gaining, more slowly. The Corps sheet (data through Tuesday midnight) has the lake at 171,370 AF, 17.1% full, up 7,247 AF since last Tuesday and up 394 on the day, well under last week’s 1,312. Millerton is 44.1% and up 2,165 AF, the only one of the group that gained. Bass Angler Inc. has Don Pedro Friday through Sunday, River Bank has McClure and Ducks Unlimited has Success, both Saturday and Sunday. Expect trailers at all three.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

Davey’s Locker’s count page lists Newport boats and attributes nothing to the island, and SoCalFishReports’ skipper-post page returned no posts when we opened it this morning.

Oxnard’s boats keep posting island-style mixed bags: Aloha Spirit listed 264 skipjack, 12 calico, 4 white seabass and a couple of yellowtail on 26 Saturday, and Island Tak 50 calico on 10 rods Monday. The landing’s own name carries “Channel Islands,” but the count service doesn’t say where those fish came from, and we won’t guess. If you’re planning an island trip, ask whoever you book with where their boats have been fishing.

Rockfish rules changed Oct. 1 for every SoCal boat, island trips included. From Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, south of Point Conception, nearshore rockfish, cabezon and greenling are closed to boat anglers, and shelf rockfish and lingcod may only be taken seaward of the 50-fathom line. Shore anglers and divers are exempt. Yellowtail, calico, bonito and tuna aren’t affected. We re-read CDFW’s groundfish summary this morning for that. Also in force as of CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice, which we did not re-read this week: yellowfin 10 a day within the 20-fish general limit, bluefin 2 on top of it, and the emergency rule from Pigeon Point south: no hooks over 1½ inches and no wire leaders for shore anglers or anyone within 1,000 yards of shore.

38°02′ N (near Point Reyes) to Pigeon Point: open Sept. 1 – Oct. 31. Counting today, Oct. 14 through Oct. 31, 18 days on the calendar, if the guideline holds out.

How many fish are left? CDFW’s Sport Ocean Salmon Tracker, read directly this morning, is updated through Sept. 30: 11,040 caught, 8,960 remaining on the shared 20,000-fish guideline, 55.2% used across all open areas. That is up from 7,585 caught through Sept. 15, so the second half of September added 3,455 fish. CDFW says estimates post after each half-month period ends, so the Oct. 1–15 figure isn’t out yet. At the late-September pace of roughly 230 fish a day, 8,960 would last about 39 days, longer than the 18 left. That is our arithmetic on one fortnight, not CDFW’s, and effort changes. CDFW’s in-season changes page and Marine Management News blog, checked this morning, list no change to the fall season. Treat Oct. 31 as a ceiling, not a promise.

Two salmon a day, any species except coho, 20-inch minimum. No filleting on the boat. North of Point Conception: two single-point, single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler.

Call the hotline the morning you go: (707) 576-3429. Inseason closures can come with only a few days’ notice. That’s what happened to San Francisco in August.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish, cabezon and greenling per day, aggregate. Within that: copper 1, canary 2, and vermilion/sunset 2. The vermilion limit is 4 only in the Northern Management Area, north of Cape Mendocino, and 2 everywhere else (re-checked this morning against CDFW’s groundfish summary, updated June 23, 2026). Lingcod 2 a day, 22-inch minimum. No retention at any time of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted.

Depths: Northern, Mendocino, San Francisco and Central areas (everything north of Point Conception) are open at all depths through Dec. 31. Southern area: nearshore species closed; shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of 50 fathoms only from Oct. 1. You must carry a descending device. Cordell Bank: all groundfish fishing is prohibited there, per CDFW. Check the boundary on CDFW’s Ocean Sport Fishing interactive map and the NMFS compliance guide before you go.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Low water on the Kings still makes people bold. The release below Pine Flat is running around 250 cfs, so you’ll see crossings and bars you couldn’t reach a few weeks ago. It’s still cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can go back up. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

Correction: the Monterey County shellfish warning is lifted. The state health department ended it Oct. 12, so last week’s “don’t eat it” no longer applies to Monterey County. The statewide sport mussel quarantine still runs through at least Oct. 31, so no mussels anywhere. Anchovy taken from Pigeon Point to Point Lobos was bait only by CDFW order since Sept. 11, and we saw no notice lifting it, so treat it as still in force. Shellfish line: (800) 553-4133.

No minus tides, but the Delta flats drain at dawn. Port Chicago’s lows run 0.14 ft at 4:40 a.m. Tuesday and 0.14 ft at 5:19 a.m. Wednesday. If you’re wading a shallow flat or running a slough in the dark, carry a real light and watch the sounder.

Young white sharks may still be close to shore. CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice set an emergency rule from Pigeon Point south that bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. We didn’t re-read the notice this week, so check CDFW before you fish that stretch. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 17.1%, Success 9.9%, Kaweah 10.3%, Eastman 6.8%, and Don Pedro, McClure and Shaver each lost thousands of acre-feet this week. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is closer to prop depth now. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind and fire season aren’t over. Check road, fire and wind before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, salmon. Pigeon Point to Point Reyes is open through Oct. 31, 18 days counting today. CDFW’s tracker now runs through Sept. 30: 11,040 caught and 8,960 left on the 20,000-fish guideline. The last half of September added about 230 fish a day, and at that pace the rest lasts about 39 days, so the calendar will probably end the season before the count does. The Bay boats put 326 kings on 208 rods over the last four days. Call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, the quiet stretch of the moon. First quarter is Sunday morning, the almanac’s slow part of the cycle, and there are no minus tides to help. What we do have is a dusk major Saturday and a dawn major Sunday on a rising tide. The full moon is Oct. 25.

Third, the lakes. Pine Flat is up 7,247 AF and still only 17.1% full, Success is under 10%, and Don Pedro, McClure and Shaver all dropped again. We also couldn’t get a usable freshwater bite report this week, so the bite table says so. Call the marina before you tow.
