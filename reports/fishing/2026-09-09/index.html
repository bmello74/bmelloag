Last week we told you the fall opener limited out and then the bite went quiet. It did — for two days. Sunday the northern fleet managed 146 kings on 186 anglers, 0.78 a rod. Monday it firmed to 206 on 191, 1.08. Then Tuesday happened: 275 kings on 145 anglers — 1.90 a rod, with eleven of fourteen boats at exactly two apiece. The bite did not recover. It tripled.

Outer Limits out of Sausalito had the fish of the week at 34 pounds, on a boat that also went 22-for-11 — limits and the big fish on the same trip. Lovely Martha put 41 on 22 rods with a 32-pounder. New Easy Rider 40 on 20. California Dawn II 36 on 18. Fish Emeryville led their Wednesday post with “RED HOT SALMON BITE YESTERDAY!!” and Allen Chin filed “outstanding limits style fishing today.” Salty Lady, on Monday, had already called it: “Get ‘em while we still can.”

The timing this week is unusually clean, and it cuts two ways. The new moon lands Friday at 1:29 p.m., which by every almanac convention puts the best five days of the month squarely inside this window. But the minus tides are leaving, not arriving — this morning and tomorrow morning are the last two below-zero lows of the cycle, and by Sunday the morning low is a lazy plus-one. If you were waiting for the deep water to drain, you waited too long. If you were waiting for the moon, you timed it perfectly.

Inland the news is thinner, and some of it is that there is no news. Pine Flat gave up another 13,651 acre-feet and is under sixteen percent. The Kings natural flow, the Tule and the Kaweah’s Dry Creek gauge all came back “not reported” this morning, and the Section 7 sheet dropped Shaver, Don Pedro, McClure and the McSwain release entirely. We are not going to invent those. Details, and what we could not get, in Water Watch.

◉ This Week’s Best Bets

 

● No. 1 — Salmon

The Bite Tripled in Three Days

Tuesday: New Easy Rider 40 kings on 20 rods. California Dawn II 36 on 18. Outer Limits 22 on 11 with a 34-pounder. Lovely Martha 41 on 22 with a 32. Pacific Pearl 20 on 10. Eleven of fourteen boats at exactly two a rod. Berkeley went four-for-four. Book it — the fall guideline is 20,000 fish shared across both open areas.

● No. 2 — Moon & Tide

New Moon Friday, and the Minus Tides Are Leaving

New moon Friday Sep 11 at 1:29 p.m. PDT. The almanac window brackets it: Wednesday through Sunday, peaking Thursday to Saturday. But the below-zero lows end tomorrow — Golden Gate is −0.47 ft this morning and −0.25 ft Thursday, then positive. Thursday is the one day this week that is both a moon day and a minus-tide day.

● No. 3 — Bottom Fish

Straight Limits, and Nobody Is Out There

Every boat that bothered came in full. Sea Wolf 240 rockfish and 48 lingcod on 24 rods — ten and two, both limits. New Huck Finn 190 and 38 on 19, same. Queen of Hearts 110 on 11. Kahuna 100 on 10. All exactly 10.00 a rod. The salmon opener pulled the fleet off the reef and the reef did not notice.

● No. 4 — San Diego

Yellowfin at Five a Rod, Overnight

Producer came off an overnight with 95 yellowfin and 25 skipjack on 19 rods — 5.00 yellowfin a rod. Old Glory 65 on 13, also 5.00. Highliner 150 yellowfin and 46 bluefin on 21 over two days. You do not need a three-day trip for this one; the overnight boats are getting it.

⚠ Water Watch — Flows & Levels

Pine Flat gave up 13,651 acre-feet in seven days and sits at 15.9% of gross pool, elevation 742.47 — down almost six feet from last Tuesday. It is releasing 1,690 cfs against 862 cfs of inflow, so it is losing roughly twice what it takes in. This is the lowest we have printed it this year.

Last week’s river reversal reversed back. The Kings below the North Fork near Trimmer is 233.1 cfs, down from 285.9. The Kern at Kernville is 184.1, down from 189.3. Both had come up the week before; both gave it back. Kernville is still the friendliest wading water on this list, but it is trending the wrong way again.

What the Corps did not report this morning. Kings River natural flow into Pine Flat: not reported. Both Tule River gauges: not reported. Dry Creek near Lemoncove: not reported. We carried a natural-flow figure last week and we cannot carry one this week. The Kaweah at Three Rivers did report, at 42.4 cfs, and McKay Point at 20.0. The Fresno River above Hensley is zero for the seventh straight week. Mill Creek near Piedra is dry.

On the Stanislaus, the Goodwin release held at 202 cfs — exactly where last week’s seven-fold cut left it. Stanislaus natural flow at New Melones is 418 cfs. Flat is good news after a drop like that; it means the operators are done moving it for now. Look at it anyway before you launch.

◉ Eastern Sierra

 

This is the week the east side earns the drive. September nights in the 30s and 40s put cold water back on top, the summer crowds are gone after Labor Day, and CDFW is still planting up here while the valley floor gets nothing. Mono County took nine separate plants this week — more than any other county in the state on this week’s list.

WaterWhat’s Going On
June Lake LoopJune, Gull, Silver and Grant. The fall pattern is on: fish shallow at first light and again the last hour, deeper and slower through the middle. Rush Creek between Silver and Grant fished well through the Loop’s plants. Grant is the big-fish water of the four.
Lee Vining CreekPlanted this week — both the main creek and the South Fork. Small water, fresh fish, easy access off 120. Best pocket water on the whole east side for somebody who wants to catch a few without a boat.
Upper Owens (above Crowley)Planted this week. This is the water that changes character in September as fish start staging out of Crowley. Special regulations apply and they are not the statewide rules — sections carry gear restrictions and different limits, and some of it shifts on the calendar. Read the sign at the access you use.
Owens — Sections 2 & 3, and below TinemahaAll three planted this week. Below Tinemaha is the lowest-elevation piece and fishes best early. Section 2 and Section 3 both took fish.
CrowleyNot on this week’s plant list, but the September perch-and-trout pattern at the north end is the reason people come. Crowley has its own regulations, including gear and bag rules that differ from statewide and that change through the season. Check before you launch, every time.
Saddlebag LakePlanted this week. At 10,000 feet this is the highest drive-to water in California and it is spectacular in September. It is also the first to get weathered out — check the road.
Mary LakePlanted this week. Mammoth Lakes basin, easy access, family water.
West Walker (Sections 2 & 3)Both sections planted this week. The road-accessible pull-offs along 395 north of Walker get hit hard on weekends; go a hundred yards either direction and you are usually alone.
Bridgeport Reservoir & the East WalkerNeither appears on this week’s plant list. Bridgeport’s big browns start moving in September and the East Walker below the dam is the marquee wild-trout water of the region. The East Walker carries special regulations — gear restrictions and a reduced limit that are not the statewide rules. This is the single most-cited regulation on the east side and the one people get wrong. Read the sign.

◉ Delta, Rivers & Aqueduct

 
WaterWhat’s Going On
The DeltaSeptember is when the Delta turns back into a striper fishery. Port Chicago is the tide station that matters here, and it has minus lows this morning (−0.12 ft at 7:53 a.m.) and tomorrow (−0.01 ft at 8:30 a.m.) — then nothing below zero for the rest of the window. Those two mornings are your best moving water. Largemouth are on the outside weed edges early; the frog bite is still worth an hour at first light before the sun gets on it. This is the busiest tournament water in the state right now — see the Tournament Board; there are nine separate permitted events on Delta water on Saturday the 19th alone.
California AqueductBank access along the O’Neill and Los Banos stretches. Stripers on cut bait and swimbaits at the checks and siphons, and the fish concentrate wherever the flow does. Early or late — there is no shade out there and the concrete radiates. Watch your footing on the liner; it is slicker than it looks and there is nothing to grab.
San Luis & O’Neill ForebayThe forebay is the more reliable of the two in September — smaller, shallower, and the stripers push bait into the corners on wind. Afternoon wind is the standing hazard here and it comes up fast enough to change your day; San Luis proper can go from fishable to genuinely dangerous inside an hour. Neither is on the Corps sheet, so we have no storage figures for you.
Upper Kings, above Pine FlatThe wild-trout water above the lake, up toward Kirch Flat and the Garnet Dike stretch. The Corps did not report Kings natural flow this morning, so we cannot tell you what is coming down — last week it was 293.3 cfs and the week before 185.5, so it has been moving. What we do have is the gauge below the North Fork near Trimmer at 233.1 cfs, down from 285.9. The upper river fishes small and technical in September; hoppers and ants, and go early. The road in is the real commitment.
Kings below Pine FlatPlanted with rainbows this week — it is on the CDFW list under Fresno, Kings and Tulare counties, which is the same tailwater showing up three times. That is the good news. The bad news is the release: 1,690 cfs. That is down from 1,742 last week and it is still not a wading flow. Fish it from the bank or from a boat, wear the vest, and do not talk yourself into one more step. Fresh fish are not worth it.
Lower Stanislaus & GoodwinGoodwin release held at 202 cfs after last week’s cut from 1,388. Stanislaus natural flow at New Melones is 418 cfs. A week of stability is genuinely useful here — the channel has had time to settle. It can also change again with no notice. Look before you launch.
Calaveras & Mokelumne sideMormon Slough at Bellota is 26.5 cfs; Cosgrove Creek near Valley Springs is zero. New Hogan is off 2,809 AF on the week and holding 163,462. Small-water bass and catfish; nothing dramatic.

Catalina & the Island Boats

One trip in this window was posted as a Santa Catalina Island trip, and here it is. The Sport King, out of LA Waterfront Cruises and Sportfishing in San Pedro, ran a ¾-day to the island on Monday September 7 with 35 anglers and came back with 12 yellowtail, 35 blue perch, 11 calico bass, 5 sheephead, 5 whitefish and 8 bonito. That is 0.34 yellowtail a rod — a grinding island day, not a hero day, and worth knowing before you book one.

From the landings’ own report pages: Aaron Graham on the Native Sun out of 22nd Street filed on September 4 that it “hasn’t been wide open but we’re getting our shots daily on this beautiful grade yellowtail, a token Mahi and lots of bonito of all sizes this week,” and noted they were sold out through Monday. The Freedom crew posted a “great day of island fishing” with a good number of yellowtail and calico — but that report is dated August 26, outside this window, and we are not going to date-launder it into this week.

What we will not do is guess. Neither count service breaks the island out, and Davey’s Locker’s own count page did not attribute a single trip in this window to Catalina — the island appears only in their site navigation. Plenty of San Pedro and Long Beach yellowtail landed this week almost certainly came from island water, and the Amigo (50 bluefin, 38 yellowtail, 15 anglers, 1.5-day) and the Eldorado (38 yellowtail, 31 anglers, overnight) are the obvious candidates. But neither boat said Catalina, so we are not saying it for them. Call 22nd Street at (310) 832-8304 or the Long Beach landings and ask where the boat is actually going tomorrow.

⏰ The Season Clock — Verified This Morning

Ocean salmon, fall season. 38°02′ N. lat. to Pigeon Point: September 1 – October 31. That is 53 open days left counting today. Pigeon Point to the U.S./Mexico border, which is Monterey and south: September 1 – 30 — 22 days left counting today. Daily bag 2 salmon of any species except coho. Minimum size 20 inches. No coho, ever. Salmon may not be filleted on the boat.

The number that actually decides when it ends: 20,000. That is the fall harvest guideline, and it applies across both open areas combined. CDFW states plainly that inseason action may close open days when harvest approaches it. We checked the CDFW Marine Management News blog this morning and the most recent salmon post is still August 21 — no inseason action has been taken on the fall season as of today. But last month federal action closed San Francisco a week early, and a fleet-wide day like Tuesday moves that counter fast. Call the hotline before you drive: (707) 576-3429.

Gear rules people get wrong. North of Point Conception: no more than two single-point, single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler when salmon are aboard. Between the 40°10′ line and Point Conception, when fishing bait by any means other than trolling, you must use two barbless circle hooks, hard-tied, no more than 5 inches apart.

Rockfish and groundfish. 10 fish per day aggregate. Within that: canary rockfish 2, copper rockfish 1, vermilion 4 north of Point Conception. Lingcod 2. No retention at all of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted rockfish — those four are the ones that end a good day badly. Know the difference between a copper and a quillback before you drop.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Pine Flat is at 15.9% and has dropped almost six more feet in seven days. That is the most dangerous number in this issue and it got worse, not better. Every rock, stump and old roadbed that was safely under your hull in August is at prop depth now, and some of the ramps are ending in dirt. Idle in, watch the sounder, and throw out every waypoint you set before this month. The lake you fished in July is not the lake that is out there.

The lower Kings is at 1,690 cfs and CDFW just planted trout in it. That combination is exactly how people get in trouble — fresh fish are a reason to get in the water and 1,690 cfs is not a wading flow. It has eased by fifty cfs in a week, which is nothing. Fish it from the bank or from a boat, wear the vest, and do not take the one more step.

Four lakes on this page have no published level at all. Shaver, Don Pedro, McClure and the McSwain release all came back empty, and there are permitted tournaments on Don Pedro and McClure inside three weeks. A blank row is not a safe row — it means nobody has told us where the water is. Call the operating agency or the marina before you tow, every time, and especially at these levels.

The minus tides end Thursday, and that is its own hazard. This morning and tomorrow morning put people out on rock, reef and mudflat that the ocean takes back fast — at Port San Luis the low is at 3:32 a.m. today, in the dark, more than three hours before sunrise. If you are out there for clams or off the rocks, know your flood time, take a light, watch the water and never turn your back on it.

Fire danger stays extreme in the high country. That is the ranger district’s wording, not ours. If you are headed to Shaver, Edison, Florence, Mammoth Pool, Wishon, Courtright, Portal Forebay or over to Saddlebag, check road and fire status before you leave and know your second way out. One closed road is a much bigger problem at 8,000 feet than it is on the valley floor.

And the heat. September in this valley is still summer no matter what the calendar says. Off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening, take more water than you think you need, take shade and a hat, and tell somebody where you went and when you will be back.
