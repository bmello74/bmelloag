Today is the last day of salmon season south of Pigeon Point. Monterey and everything south of it closes tonight. From Pigeon Point up to Point Reyes the season stays open through Oct. 31, 32 days counting today, unless CDFW closes it early when the shared 20,000-fish guideline fills. As of this morning CDFW has posted no inseason action on the fall season. We could not read a tracker update past Sept. 15, so we can’t tell you how many fish are left. Neither can the landings. Fish Emeryville’s own words Tuesday: “Book before quota is met!”

The fish haven’t gotten the memo. Saturday through Tuesday the northern party boats reported 409 kings on 347 rods. On Monday only three boats went, and all three came back with limits: California Dawn II with 28 on 14 and fish to 27 pounds, Hog Heaven with 22 on 11 and fish to 28.

Closer to home, the Kings got a lot friendlier. Tuesday at 8 a.m. the Corps dropped the Pine Flat release from 700 cfs to 355 at the request of the Kings River Water Master. That is half the water, and less than half the 890 cfs it hit a week ago. CDFW also planted the river below the dam for a third straight week.

Two rule changes before you book a boat. Starting tomorrow, Oct. 1, south of Point Conception the shallow-water rockfish, cabezon and greenling season closes, and lingcod and shelf rockfish can only be taken deeper than 50 fathoms. North of Point Conception nothing changes; it’s open at all depths through December. We also owe you a correction on the vermilion limit. It’s in the Season Clock below.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat turned the tap way down. After peaking at 890 cfs last Wednesday afternoon, the release stepped down through the weekend to 700, then dropped to 355 cfs at 8 a.m. Tuesday. The Corps’ note says Mill and Hughes creeks are at zero, so what comes out of the dam is the river. With less going out, the lake gained 732 acre-feet Tuesday. It still lost 555 AF on the week and sits at 157,506 AF, 15.8%. Kings natural flow read 215.1 cfs Tuesday, down from 263.6 a week ago; below the North Fork near Trimmer, 203.5.

The south Valley lakes keep dropping. Isabella lost 3,960 AF, to 26.5%. Success gave up another 1,515, to 13.1%, and Kaweah 734, to 10.9%. Eastman (6.8%) and Hensley (7.4%) barely moved because there’s almost nothing left to move.

The Section 7 sheet went backwards. This morning’s “current” non-Corps report is a run with data ending Sept. 14, older than the numbers we printed last week. So there is no fresh level for Millerton, Don Pedro, McClure, New Melones, Shaver, Bass Lake or the Kaiser Pass lakes. Below we repeat the newest figures that were ever published, with their dates, and show no trend. BASS 101 runs Pine Flat Saturday and Millerton Sunday. If you’re towing to a lake on that list, call the marina first.

Wishon & Courtright On no Corps sheet, and we found no current report we could date. High-country trout water that fishes into October, weather allowing. Call ahead on access and the seasonal gates before you make the drive.

◉ Eastern Sierra

 

First dated report from the Bridgeport end in weeks. Carolyn Webb at Virginia Lakes Resort filed Monday, Sept. 28: aspens turning gold around the resort, the lake stocked Friday, and a 5-pound, 15-ounce rainbow on Sept. 26 in the resort’s photo caption. In her words, “crawlers took over the top spot with power bait close behind.” Pink or orange with white heads, garlic glitter and yellow glitter PowerBait all worked. On hardware, stay small: 1/6-oz Buoyants, gold Kastmasters, a gold Super Duper. She says the water has warmed up again, so go deep. Fly-and-bubble with small dark flies works best early and late.

Two notes from the same report. Big Virginia is a drinking-water lake: no swimming, no gas motors, and no float tubes or boards without full waders. And high winds knocked out power on both sides of Conway Summit last Friday. Nothing new was posted this week from Crowley, the Owens or the June Lake Loop, and no Eastern Sierra plant had been posted for the week of Sept. 27–Oct. 3 as of this morning.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

Wading: easier doesn’t mean safe. It’s cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the Water Master can raise the release again with little notice. Check the flow before you step in.

San Joaquin below Friant No current flow published; it’s on the stale Section 7 sheet. The last number we trust is 375 cfs on Sept. 17. Don’t plan around it.
California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay No verified current report. The usual fall pattern: catfish on cut bait and dip bait along the canal, stripers in the boils and around the check gates early. This warning holds whatever the reports say: aqueduct water is swift, cold and steep-sided, with no way out. Wear a PFD, fish with somebody, and stay off wet concrete.

Catalina & the Island Boats

Nobody named Catalina in writing again this week, so neither will we. Both 22nd Street reports posted since last Wednesday were from the Freedom, and both were offshore tuna trips. Sept. 26: “Today we went to check out a diffrent zone and it payed off” for 65 yellowfin. Then Capt. Alex Natipadab’s 1.5-day came in with 10 bluefin and 85 yellowfin. Pierpoint’s Enterprise (Capt. Andy Siratt) wrote Sept. 27 that “things seem to be getting back on track on the local scene,” and on the 28th listed tuna, yellowtail, marlin, barracuda, bass and bonito. It didn’t say where. Davey’s Locker’s count page attributes nothing to the island.

22nd Street’s full-day Pursuit keeps posting island-style mixed bags: 18 yellowtail, 25 calico, 44 bonito and 10 barracuda on 39 Tuesday, and 13 yellowtail plus 142 rockfish on 40 Monday. The count service doesn’t say where those fish came from, and we won’t guess. Ask the landing: (310) 832-8304.

Rockfish rules change tomorrow for every SoCal boat, island trips included. From Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, south of Point Conception, nearshore rockfish, cabezon and greenling are closed to boat anglers, and shelf rockfish and lingcod may only be taken seaward of the 50-fathom line. Shore anglers and divers are exempt. Yellowtail, calico, bonito and tuna aren’t affected. Also still in force: yellowfin 10 a day within the 20-fish general limit, bluefin 2, and the emergency rule from Pigeon Point south: no hooks over 1½ inches and no wire leaders for shore anglers or anyone within 1,000 yards of shore.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish, cabezon and greenling per day, aggregate. Within that: copper 1, canary 2, and vermilion/sunset 2. The vermilion limit is 4 only in the Northern Management Area, from the Oregon line to Cape Mendocino. Lingcod 2 a day, 22-inch minimum. No retention at any time of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted.

Depths from Oct. 1: Northern, Mendocino, San Francisco and Central areas (everything north of Point Conception) stay open at all depths through Dec. 31. Southern area: nearshore species closed; shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of 50 fathoms only. Cordell Bank stays closed to all groundfish, and you must carry a descending device.

Correction: Our last several issues said vermilion was 4 a day north of Point Conception. CDFW’s groundfish summary (updated June 23, 2026) says 4 applies only north of Cape Mendocino. From Morro Bay to Bodega Bay, the limit is 2. We had it wrong, and we’re sorry.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

 

Phase computed from the synodic month (29.530588853 days) against the reference new moon of Jan. 6, 2000, 18:14 UTC, and checked against the moon’s true position. Illumination is at local noon. Almanac convention: the best fishing brackets the new and the full moon.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Lower water on the Kings makes people bold. At 355 cfs you’ll see crossings and bars you couldn’t reach last week, and fresh planters are sitting in the deep runs on the other side. It’s still cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can go back up. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

The minus tides are all night tides. Tonight’s Golden Gate low comes at 9:00, Thursday’s at 10:04, and by Sunday they’re after midnight. If you walk a drained flat or reef for clams or bait, carry a real light, know your way back before you walk out, and start back before the turn.

Young white sharks are still close to shore. The emergency rule from Pigeon Point south still bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 15.8%, Success 13.1%, Kaweah 10.9%, Eastman 6.8%. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is at prop depth now. Millerton, Don Pedro, McClure, New Melones and Bass Lake have no current published level at all. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind and fire season aren’t over. High winds knocked out power around Conway Summit last Friday. Check road, fire and wind before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, salmon. If you fish out of Monterey or Santa Cruz, today is your last day. If you fish out of the Bay, the calendar gives you through Oct. 31, but the guideline may not. We couldn’t verify how many fish are left, and we won’t guess. The boats that went Monday all came back with limits. If you want one more king, go soon, and call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, the Kings. Half the water and a third straight week of plants. That’s the best setup on the lower river this fall. Saturday’s major lands right at sunrise, so if you only get one morning, make it that one.

Third, a correction. We’ve been printing the vermilion rockfish limit as 4 north of Point Conception. CDFW says 4 applies only north of Cape Mendocino. From there south to Mexico, it’s 2. If you kept a third or fourth vermilion on our say-so, that one’s on us. We fixed it above.
