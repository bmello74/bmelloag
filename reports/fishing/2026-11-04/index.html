Recreational Dungeness crab opens Saturday, Nov. 7, but with the traps restricted. CDFW’s Oct. 30 release has the season opening in most areas that day. From the California/Oregon border to Point Conception, recreational crab traps are restricted until at least Jan. 1, 2027 because of whale entanglements this year; hoop nets and crab snares are not affected, and hoop nets must be serviced every two hours. A stretch of the north coast, Cape Mendocino (40°10′ N) to the Ten Mile State Marine Reserve (39°33.3′ N), stays closed over domoic acid in the crab. The commercial season is delayed to at least Jan. 1, and CDFW’s next risk assessment is mid-December. Fish Emeryville’s own posts confirm crab combos start the 7th, and say its last trips that strictly target lingcod run Nov. 4–5.

Salmon is over. The Pigeon Point to Point Reyes season ran through Saturday, Oct. 31; south of Pigeon Point it closed Sept. 30. CDFW’s salmon page, read this morning, lists no later action, and the newest salmon item on its Marine Management News blog is still Aug. 21. The count service listed only two salmon trips in the last two days: Oakland Anglers II with 5 kings on 15 Friday and Argo with 3 on 4 Saturday, 8 on 19 rods, 0.42 a rod. The count service may not list every boat. We did not re-read CDFW’s catch tracker, which when we last read it ran through Oct. 15 at 13,769 caught of 20,000, so we print no final tally.

Lingcod and rockfish are the steady bite, and San Diego’s yellowfin are still coming. Surf Scooter (Bodega Bay) listed 42 lingcod to 23 lb and 210 rockfish on 21 rods Sunday, limits both ways, and New Huck Finn (Emeryville) had the same 42 and 210 on 21 Saturday. Morro Bay’s Starfire had 220 rockfish on 22. In San Diego, the Seaforth full-day boat San Diego listed 208 yellowfin on 36 rods Tuesday, 5.78 a rod, Sea Adventure 80 105 yellowfin and 90 skipjack on 16 rods on a 2-day trip, and New Lo-An 120 yellowfin to 109 pounds on 12 rods on a 2.5-day trip Sunday. Newport’s tuna bite has gone quiet: Freelance had 137 yellowfin on 56 Saturday and 8 yellowtail on 62 Tuesday.

The new moon is Sunday night, Nov. 8, at 11:02 p.m., and the minus tides come back with it. The Golden Gate low goes below zero Saturday and bottoms at −0.54 ft at 5:24 p.m. Monday, a 6.73 ft drop from the 10:35 a.m. high, with the lows landing around sunset instead of after dark. Clocks already changed Sunday, so everything below is standard time. Pine Flat is up again, +3,385 acre-feet to 18.5%, Millerton gained 4,691, and the sport-mussel quarantine ended at midnight Oct. 31 (CDPH, Oct. 28 release). The permit database lists five bass events on Millerton and Don Pedro Saturday and Sunday, so expect company at those ramps.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat is up again, and the Kings release was cut. The Corps sheet (data through Tuesday midnight, generated 7 a.m. today) has the lake at 184,597 AF, 18.5% full, up 3,385 AF since last Tuesday against last week’s gain of 3,542, and up 675 on Tuesday alone. The Corps’ hourly report has the release at 149 to 150 cfs since Tuesday morning, down from the 196 to 201 we printed last week. The Kings below the North Fork, at the head of the lake, reads 274 to 286 cfs over the last 24 hours, and the sheet’s computed inflow swings from negative to over 1,000 cfs hour to hour; the Corps itself warns that figure is erratic, so the storage gain comes from the elevation gauge.

A small rise came through the south Valley rivers. Kaweah at Three Rivers (CDEC daily mean) ran 63 cfs on Oct. 30, 116 on Sunday, 135 on Monday and 104 on Tuesday; our data doesn’t say why. Kaweah still lost 530 AF, to 10.3%, while Success gained 300 to 10.1% and Eastman edged up 7 to 6.8%. Isabella lost 210 AF, to 24.5%, and is now releasing 322 cfs against 227 coming in, up from 269 last week. Hensley gained 162 AF to 7.5%. Merced near Snelling is down to 212 cfs from 275.

Millerton is the surprise. It gained 4,691 AF to 43.4% after losing 5,752 the week before. The rest of the foothill lakes have nearly stopped dropping: New Melones 62.7%, down only 279 AF on the Corps’ sheet, and Don Pedro 72.6% through Tuesday, down 608 AF in eight days (CDEC). McClure is 49.0%, down 5,515 AF in nine days, and Shaver (49.5%) and Huntington (61,671 AF) are still falling, by 2,019 and 4,582 AF in seven days. Stale numbers are footnoted below.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

 

Illumination is the moon’s true figure at local noon, and the new-moon time, sunrise, sunset and lunar transits come from an astronomy-library calculation (ephem) for Fresno (36.75°N, 119.77°W); we cross-checked the phase against the 29.530589-day synodic month from the Jan. 6, 2000 new moon, and the mean calculation lands about 15 hours after the true new moon, which is normal. Local time is standard time all window. Solunar majors are the moon overhead and underfoot, in the order they fall in the day. Almanac convention: the best fishing brackets the new and the full moon.

This is a bracket week. The moon was last quarter Sunday, Nov. 1, and is new at about 11:02 p.m. Sunday, Nov. 8; first quarter is Nov. 17 and full moon Nov. 24. By the almanac’s convention the best fishing runs from about Nov. 6 through Nov. 10, and the tide agrees: the biggest swings of the window, 6.69 and 6.73 ft at the Golden Gate on Sunday and Monday, land on those days.

Where the majors fall. Near the new moon the moon crosses overhead near midday and underfoot near midnight, so the majors fall around noon and midnight, not at dawn or dusk: Sunday’s are 11:14 a.m. and 11:38 p.m., Monday’s only one, 12:02 p.m. Sunrise is 6:30 a.m. and sunset 4:55 p.m., so on Sunday and Monday the tide, not the moon clock, sets the best hour: the ebb runs out through the afternoon, and the minus low (4:48 p.m. Sunday, 5:24 p.m. Monday) comes at dusk. The midday majors, with the ebb running hard, are the other window.

⚠ Water & Weather Safety

Minus tides mean fast water at dusk. From Saturday the Golden Gate drains more than 6 feet in under seven hours, to −0.45 ft at 4:48 p.m. Sunday and −0.54 ft at 5:24 p.m. Monday. The flood comes back just as fast over rocks, jetties and bars you walked out to on the ebb, and from Monday the turn comes after sunset. If you’re on the rocks, a flat or a sandbar, know when the tide turns, carry a real light, and give yourself a way back before it does.

Dark comes early. The clocks changed Sunday, and sunset runs about 4:52 to 4:58 p.m. this window. Plan your launch and your drive home around it.

Crab season brings whales into the conversation. CDFW has a fleet advisory for every zone: keep gear out of areas where whales are present. Traps are restricted from the Oregon border to Point Conception, so if you set a hoop net, tend it every two hours and don’t leave it unattended. Cape Mendocino to Ten Mile State Marine Reserve is closed over domoic acid; the closure line is (831) 649-2883.

The Kings release is low, and still cold. The Corps’ hourly report had the flow below Pine Flat at 149 to 150 cfs since Tuesday morning, and the water at the head of the lake at 53 to 57°F. It’s cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can change overnight. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

Shellfish: the mussel quarantine is over, but advisories remain. CDPH’s Oct. 28 release ended the statewide sport-harvested mussel quarantine at midnight Oct. 31. It says the Northern Channel Islands advisory for sport-harvested mussels, clams, scallops and oysters stays in place over domoic acid, as do the Del Norte and Humboldt County razor clam notices. Check CDPH before you gather anything. Shellfish line: (800) 553-4133. We did not re-read CDFW’s Sept. 11 order making anchovy taken from Pigeon Point to Point Lobos bait only, and saw no notice lifting it, so treat it as still in force.

Young white sharks may still be close to shore. CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice set an emergency rule from Pigeon Point south that bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. We didn’t re-read the notice this week, so check CDFW before you fish that stretch. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 18.5%, Success 10.1%, Kaweah 10.3%, Eastman 6.8%, Hensley 7.5%, and Shaver and Huntington are still losing water. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is closer to prop depth now. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind, fire and the first snow. Check road, fire, wind and snow before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, the crab. Recreational Dungeness opens Saturday, Nov. 7, with traps restricted from the Oregon border to Point Conception until at least Jan. 1 and Cape Mendocino to Ten Mile closed over domoic acid. That’s CDFW’s Oct. 30 release, not a landing’s say-so. Salmon is done for 2026; the last two days the count service listed were 8 kings on 19 rods. If you’re planning ahead, the salmon hotline is (707) 576-3429.

Second, the new moon. It’s Sunday night, Nov. 8, and the Golden Gate goes to −0.54 ft Monday at 5:24 p.m. Minus tides at dusk mean water coming back fast over the rocks. Carry a light and mind the early dark.

Third, the lakes. Pine Flat is up 3,385 AF to 18.5% full, Millerton gained 4,691, and Don Pedro and New Melones have nearly stopped dropping, while McClure, Shaver and Huntington are still falling. This week’s trout plant list is only Bass Lake and Donner Lake. We also couldn’t get a usable freshwater bite report, so the bite table says so. Call the marina before you tow.
