The bottom is still the best thing going. Monday and Tuesday the boats out of Berkeley, Emeryville, Half Moon Bay and Monterey came in with 330, 260, 230, 210, 150 and 130 rockfish — limits on every one of those trips — and the lingcod are riding along with them: 56, 42 and 12 on three of those same runs. If you want a full sack and a short argument about it, that is your week.

The salmon kept sliding. Across the ten salmon trips posted Monday and Tuesday, two came in at limits — Bodega’s Dragonfly and Emeryville’s Scallywag, both six-rod loads. Everything else ran between half a fish and one and a third per rod. And the clock is short: San Francisco and Monterey are the only two areas still open, and both close August 31 — thirteen days from this morning.

Two things changed inland. The fall striped bass run is on in the Delta — the first guide reports we have had out of Central California in a month, and they are good ones. And on the water side, Pine Flat has now dropped 10.2 feet in eight days while the lower Kings release went back up. That reverses three straight weeks of cuts. Details below.

Two sections in this issue are here because you asked for them. The Eastern Sierra coverage — June Lake Loop up through Bridgeport — and the Southern California dock totals covering San Pedro, Long Beach, Newport, Dana Point and San Diego, along with the Catalina block, both went in after readers wrote in and requested them. Keep it coming: there is a button near the bottom of this issue for telling us what water we are still missing.

◉ This Week’s Best Bets

 

● No. 1 — Ocean

Rockfish and Lingcod, Still Limits

California Dawn II 330 rockfish on 33 rods Monday. New California Dawn 260 rockfish and 56 lingcod on 26. New Huck Finn 210 and 42 ling Tuesday. Queen of Hearts 230 on 23. Every one of those is ten a rod. This is the most dependable fishery on the coast right now.

● No. 2 — Delta

The Fall Striper Run Has Started

Alan Fong is taking linesides to 13 pounds on topwater in the shallows at Liberty Island and moving west toward Sherman Lake. Water temperature has dropped about eight degrees. Frank’s Tract, the Shoals and Prisoner’s Point are all holding fish. First real Delta report we have had in a month.

● No. 3 — Central Sierra

Kokanee at Shaver and New Melones

Dick Nichols took nearly four limits of kokanee on one Shaver trip, running pink at 80 to 90 feet. Kyle Wise is finding limits of up to 20 kokanee by 10 a.m. at New Melones over island tops at 90 feet. Fish are in full spawn colors. Pressure is heavy at Shaver ahead of the Sept 7 derby.

● No. 4 — Moon & Tide

Wait for the Back Half

First quarter lands Thursday morning — the flattest tides of the month. Today’s range at the Gate is 2.7 feet. It builds every day after that to a 6.0-foot high Tuesday night and the first minus tides since Aug 14. Full moon is Thursday the 27th. Front half: skip it or fish the bottom. Back half: plan around it.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat: 10.2 feet in eight days. From 770.68 ft and 229,111 acre-feet on August 10 to 760.47 ft and 202,375 acre-feet at midnight Tuesday — 26,736 acre-feet gone. The lake is now 20.2% of gross pool, and it is still coming off at roughly 2,400 acre-feet a day. Every ramp, every rockpile and every waypoint you set in July is wrong.

And the lower Kings went back up. After three straight weeks of cuts — 4,226, then 3,071, then 2,003 cfs — the Pine Flat release is back to 2,174 cfs. We told you last week that one more cut would change the conversation. It did not come. Still bank or boat below the dam, still not a wading flow, and do not take last week’s trend line as a promise.

Above the dam it is thinning too. Kings natural flow into Pine Flat is down to 422 cfs from 587 a week ago, and the Kern at Kernville has slipped to 207.6 cfs from 223.7 — the first drop there in three weeks. Kernville is still the most comfortable wading water on our list. It is just no longer rising.

Two small pieces of good news. Hensley’s change columns, which were physically impossible last week, are back to sane values this cycle — storage off 3 acre-feet on 3 cfs of inflow. And Nacimiento and Lopez both refreshed to today after weeks of stale readings. Nacimiento is holding 285,131 acre-feet at 109% of normal for the date; Lopez 48,679 at 129%.

⚠ Before You Drive Over — Different Rulebook

Special regulations are the norm over there, not the exception. Individual Eastern Sierra waters carry their own gear restrictions, size limits, reduced bag limits and seasonal closures. Several run barbless-only or catch-and-release stretches, and Crowley in particular fishes under tighter August rules than the statewide standard — restricted baits and a reduced limit with a size minimum. Look up the specific water in the current CDFW freshwater regulations before you launch. Do not assume the five-fish valley rulebook travels with you.

Getting there from the valley: Tioga Pass (Highway 120) is the direct route to the June Lake Loop and it is a seasonal road — open now, but check Caltrans before an early start. Figure roughly five hours from Fresno to June Lake, six to Bridgeport. Sonora Pass (108) and Monitor Pass (89) are the northern alternatives.

Altitude is real. Saddlebag and Tioga sit above 10,000 feet. If you drove up from 300 feet that morning, take it slow, drink more water than you think you need, and do not plan a hike-in on day one.

◉ Delta, Rivers & Aqueduct

The Delta — The Fall Run Is On

This is the section that changed most this week. Striped bass are flooding back into the Delta. Alan Fong has been taking linesides to 13 pounds on topwater in the shallows at Liberty Island and is moving west toward Sherman Lake. On the bass side he is flipping Brush Hogs in green pumpkin or watermelon red, and D-Bombs in Delta Red, on a one-ounce weight in one to two feet of water: “The current is ripping, and the northern largemouth bass are hunkering down on the shallows. They are transitioning to wood as the water temperature has dropped around 8 degrees.”

Johnny Wang at Turner’s in Stockton puts the stripers on the San Joaquin side at the Shoals, Frank’s Tract and Prisoner’s Point, with mostly undersized fish in downtown Stockton. South of the Mossdale Bridge, Omega Nguyen reports the river loaded with threadfin and the best window two hours before high tide into the peak — blood worms, pile worms or fresh shad, with the larger fish coming on shad. Randy Pringle’s read from running two tournaments last weekend: wind is your friend. Spinnerbaits and bladed jigs when it blows, prop baits worked with long pauses when it lays flat, and flip wood with big profiles on the high tide.

On the tide book, the front half of this window is a proper neap at Port Chicago — lows of 0.9, 0.8 and 0.6 ft, highs barely clearing 3.6. By Monday the lows are back to 0.1 ft and Wednesday the 26th brings a 4.9 ft high at 12:44 a.m. against a 0.0 ft low at 8:22 a.m. That is the swing worth planning around.

Sturgeon news worth knowing. The Fish and Game Commission voted 3–2 at its August 15 meeting to allow catch-and-release only for white sturgeon, with the fishery expected to reopen within about three weeks. No harvest. The opening date is not set — it depends on the Office of Administrative Law. Do not put a card in your pocket on the strength of this paragraph; confirm with CDFW that the regulation is actually in effect before you fish.

California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay

San Luis and O’Neill are Reclamation and DWR facilities and appear on neither Corps sheet, so we still have no independently measured storage here. What we do have is people on the water. Josh Mesa reports the main lake kicking out limits of striped bass from the banks around Dinosaur Point on jumbo minnows, anchovies or blood worms, and the forebay slow because the grass is thick and limiting bank access. Roger George called the week tough for trollers between wind storms — seven fish to 23 inches on a guided trip — but then went scouting and landed a 44-inch, 30-pound striper on electric scales, his biggest of the year, released with a Seaqualizer. Water temperature 74 to 78 degrees.

In the aqueduct proper, Bill Sterling reports tough daytime action in the northern reaches and says it will be a month before temperatures cooperate — but plenty of largemouth in the local irrigation canals on crankbaits and plastic worms. Omega Nguyen adds a decent morning striper bite at Clifton Court Forebay on fresh shad. In the south aqueduct in Kern County, striper action has improved with cooler water — cut sardines, anchovies or jumbo minnows.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety — Late August

Pine Flat has dropped ten feet in eight days. That is the most dangerous number in this issue. Rock, stump and old roadbed that were well under your hull two weeks ago are at prop depth now, and ramps are ending in dirt. Idle in, watch the sounder, and throw out every waypoint you set before August.

The lower Kings went back up to 2,174 cfs. If you were waiting for it to drop into wading range, stop waiting. Bank or boat below the dam, and wear the vest.

Fire danger is extreme in the high country. That is the ranger district’s wording, not ours. If you are headed to Edison, Florence, Mammoth Pool, Wishon or Courtright, check road and fire status before you leave and know your second way out.

Eastern Sierra? Different risks. Altitude above 10,000 feet at Saddlebag and Tioga, afternoon thunderstorms that build fast over the crest, and no cell coverage in the canyons. Tell somebody your route and be off exposed water when the clouds stack up.

Heat. Valley afternoons are still the real risk on this list. Off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening. Water, shade, a hat, and somebody who knows where you went.

Two things to act on. If you want one more king before the season shuts, you have thirteen days, and the honest read is that it is getting harder, not easier — two limit trips out of ten. And if you would rather fill a sack than chase one, the rockfish and lingcod bite is the best it has been all year and it is not fussy about the tide.

Inland, the news is the Delta. The fall striper run is on, the water has cooled about eight degrees, and for the first time in a month we have real reports from people who were actually out there. Save the ocean plans for the back half of the week when the tide finally builds — but the Delta is worth a morning right now.
