Last week we told you nobody outside CDFW knew how much of the fall salmon guideline was gone. Now we do. CDFW’s Sport Ocean Salmon Tracker, updated through Sept. 15, puts the fall catch at 7,585 Chinook, with 12,415 left on the 20,000-fish guideline. That is 38% of the season’s fish gone in the first fifteen days. If the back half of September ran at the same pace, the guideline would fill around Oct. 10, three weeks before the calendar says the season ends. That is our arithmetic, not a CDFW forecast, and the pace will not hold exactly. But it is the reason we keep saying: do not plan on late October.

The fishing itself is still worth the drive. Sunday the northern fleet put 164 kings on 125 rods, with New Easy Rider at 31 on 16 and three more boats at exactly two a rod. It softened early this week, 0.88 Monday and 0.76 Tuesday on light boat counts, but over the four days it averaged just over a fish a rod. And south of Pigeon Point, the season ends Wednesday, Sept. 30, eight days counting today.

Saturday is the full moon, and this time the almanac and the clock agree. Friday through Sunday the moon sets within minutes of sunrise and rises within minutes of sunset. On the coast, the bigger tides that come with it are back: minus lows return at the Golden Gate Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday evenings, down to −0.26 ft, after a week with none. Morro Bay is making the most of it already: Endeavor brought in limits for 30 anglers plus 44 lingcod to 16 pounds on Saturday.

Inland, two things to know. The Pine Flat release we celebrated last week has crept back up from 571 cfs to 756 in four days, and CDFW planted the lower Kings a second week running. Good fish, pushier water. And the Section 7 sheet was patchy again: this morning’s run blanked Don Pedro, McClure, Millerton and New Melones, but a late-night run published most of them through Monday. We print the newest number that exists and tell you how old it is.

Bet No. 4 · Fresno, Kings & Tulare

The Lower Kings, Planted Again

CDFW planted rainbows below Pine Flat for the second straight week, week of Sept. 20–26, in all three counties the river runs through. The catch: the release rose from 571 cfs to 756 between Friday and Tuesday. Still fishable from the bank with crawlers, eggs and small spinners behind structure. Not a week to wade across anything.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat turned the tap back up. Daily outflow ran 626 cfs last Tuesday, bottomed at 571 on Friday, then climbed every day to 756 cfs Tuesday, up 32% in four days. Storage gave up 935 acre-feet on the week, to 158,061 AF, 15.8% of gross pool. Kings natural flow read 263.6 cfs Sunday, the last day it was reported; below the North Fork near Trimmer, 203.5 cfs.

The Kaiser Pass lakes are draining hard. Florence fell from 12,085 AF to 6,722, down 44% in a week. Edison dropped 17%, to 29,893, and Mammoth Pool gave up 2,956 AF. That water is headed down the San Joaquin. If you launch at Florence or Edison, expect a longer walk to the water than two weeks ago. Isabella lost another 5,058 AF, to 27.2%.

How fresh are the numbers? This morning’s Section 7 sheet published “No Project Data Available” for Don Pedro, McClure, Millerton and New Melones and nothing for the upper San Joaquin lakes. A run posted at 11:01 last night, with data through Monday, Sept. 21, did carry Don Pedro, McClure, Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence and the McSwain release; those are the figures below, marked ‡. Millerton, New Melones and Bass Lake have published nothing newer than Thursday, Sept. 17; they are starred. There are tournaments on Don Pedro and McClure again this Saturday. Call the marina.

Same straight word as last week. The weekly regional aggregator again filed “no reports for this week” for Central California, Central Sierra, Sierra Foothills and the Delta. A syndicated Central California column dated “Sept. 18–24” turned up in search; its lake levels contradict this morning’s Corps sheet on every line (it has Pine Flat at 40%; the Corps has 15.8%) and it refers to 2025 in the future tense. ◉ Eastern Sierra

 

This week’s plants moved down the Owens. CDFW stocked rainbows in the Upper Owens, Section 3 above Crowley, and the Owens below Tinemaha Reservoir, week of Sept. 20–26. Last week’s fish went into the Virginia Lakes, Convict Creek and Rock Creek; they are still in there.

The newest dated guide report we found is Sierra Drifters’ from Sept. 16, a week old. It had Crowley fish “on the chew” in McGee Bay, Layton Springs and Green Banks at 8 to 10 feet, with a lot of water between the good spots, so sonar matters. It put the Lower Owens around 500 cfs, too high to wade, and the East Walker near 230 cfs, a good wading flow. It also said the June Lake basin is turning over into fall mode, with bigger browns expected. Nothing filed out of Bridgeport or the June Lake Loop itself.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. “I checked the statewide rules” does not cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still have not confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we are not printing one. If you are planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your specific water in the current regulations booklet.

Catalina & the Island Boats

No skipper put a fish on Catalina in writing this week, so neither will we. We read every 22nd Street Landing report posted since last Wednesday and the Davey’s Locker count page for the same days. All of 22nd Street’s posts were offshore tuna trips. The Freedom crew filed on Sept. 19 with “100 yellowfin tuna weighing anywhere from 10 to 65 pounds” on an overnight, and Michael Potvin brought the Pride in Sept. 16 from a 1.5-day with 81 yellowfin, 25 dorado and a marlin. Neither named the island.

The boat most likely to have been there is 22nd Street’s Pursuit, which the landing’s schedule had on Catalina full-day trips through Sept. 24 when we checked last week. Its Monday count: 9 yellowtail, 18 calico, 66 bonito and a barracuda on 39 anglers. That is an island-style mixed bag, but the count service does not say where it was caught, and we are not going to guess for you. Call the landing at (310) 832-8304 and ask what the Pursuit is running this week.

Worth knowing before any SoCal trip: CDFW’s Marine Region posted a reminder on Sept. 17 about this year’s warmwater fish. Yellowfin are 10 a day within the 20-fish general limit, bluefin 2, and skipjack have no limit. Tuna filleted on a boat south of Point Conception must be cut in six skin-on pieces and bagged by species. From Pigeon Point south, shore anglers and anyone within 1,000 yards of shore can’t use hooks over 1½ inches or wire leaders, under an emergency white shark rule.

⏳ The Season Clock

Salmon: 12,415 fish left. 8 days south of Pigeon Point.

38°02′ N (near Point Reyes) to Pigeon Point: open Sept. 1 – Oct. 31. Counting today, 39 days on the calendar.
Pigeon Point to the U.S./Mexico border: open Sept. 1 – 30. Counting today, 8 days left. Last day is Wednesday.

The tracker, updated through Sept. 15: 7,585 Chinook estimated caught in the fall season across all open areas, 12,415 remaining on the shared 20,000-fish guideline. CDFW posts estimates by half-month, so the Sept. 16–30 number is not out yet, and says updates may come more often as the catch nears the guideline. At the first half’s pace, about 506 fish a day, the remainder runs out around Oct. 10. That projection is ours, not CDFW’s. Fewer boats are running and the south closes on the 30th, so the pace should ease. But nobody should be booking an Oct. 30 salmon trip on the assumption it will still be open.

Two salmon a day, any species except coho, 20-inch minimum. No filleting on the boat. North of Point Conception: two single-point single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler. CDFW’s most recent inseason notice is still the Aug. 21 post; no action has been taken on the fall season as of this morning.

Call the hotline the morning you go: (707) 576-3429. Inseason closures can come with a few days’ notice. It happened to San Francisco in August.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish per day, aggregate. Within that: canary 2, copper 1, vermilion 4 north of Point Conception. No retention at all of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted. Depth rules and sublimits can change at the turn of a month, and October is next week. Confirm with CDFW before your first October trip.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

 

Phase computed from the synodic month (29.530588853 days) against the reference new moon of Jan. 6, 2000, 18:14 UTC. Sunrise, sunset, moonrise and moonset computed for Fresno (36.75°N, 119.77°W), PDT. Solunar majors are the moon overhead and underfoot, listed in the order they fall in the day. Almanac convention: the best fishing brackets the new and the full moon.

This is the good week. The moon is full Saturday, Sept. 26, mid-morning: about 7:50 a.m. by the mean synodic month, 9:49 a.m. by the moon’s true position. That puts the whole almanac bracket, Thursday through Monday, inside this issue’s window, with the weekend in the middle of it.

The timing lines up too, just not on the majors. Around a full moon the majors fall near midnight and midday, good for night catfish and stripers and for the noon tide, not for first light. The minors are what land on the light: Saturday the moon sets at 6:49 a.m. against a 6:50 sunrise and rises at 6:43 p.m. against a 6:49 sunset. Friday it sets at 5:44 a.m. and rises at 6:17 p.m.; Sunday, 7:56 a.m. and 7:11 p.m. That is moonset and sunrise within a minute of each other, and moonrise and sunset within six. Fish Saturday, both ends of the day. On the coast, Saturday’s midday major falls within an hour of the Golden Gate’s 5.69-ft noon high.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

The Kings is rising again, and that is when people get caught. The release went from 571 cfs Friday to 756 Tuesday, and there are fresh planted trout in it for the second week running. A gravel bar or crossing that felt easy last weekend has more water on it now, and it is still cold, pushy tailwater over slick cobble. Wear the vest. Use a staff. If you are by yourself, fish from the bank.

This week’s minus tides fall after dark. Monday through Wednesday the Golden Gate lows come at 7:18, 8:06 and 9:00 p.m., well after sunset. If you are out on a drained flat or reef for clams or bait, carry a real light, know your way back before you walk out, and start back before the turn. The flood behind a minus low on a big-range tide comes in fast.

Young white sharks are close to shore this year. CDFW says juveniles are moving farther north than usual and staying nearshore longer. That is why the emergency gear rule from Pigeon Point south bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore. If you hook one, cut the line. Do not try to land it.

Low lakes and falling lakes. Pine Flat at 15.8%, Kaweah 11.3%, Eastman 6.9%: every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is at prop depth now. Up high, Florence lost 44% of its water in a week and Edison 17%. Ramps that worked two weekends ago may end in mud. Idle in, watch the sounder, and throw out old waypoints.

A blank row is not a safe row. Millerton, New Melones and Bass Lake have published no level since Thursday. If you are towing to any of them, or to Don Pedro or McClure for Saturday’s events, call the marina or the operating agency first.

Fire and heat have not read the calendar. The equinox was Tuesday, but fire danger in the high country is not over. Check road and fire status before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. On the valley floor, fish early or late, carry more water than you think you need, and tell somebody where you went and when you will be back.

Three things this week. First, the salmon math. For a month we have said nobody knew how much of the fall guideline was left. Now CDFW says 12,415 fish as of Sept. 15. At the first half’s pace that runs out around Oct. 10. That is our projection, not theirs, and the pace will ease as boats stop running. But if you want a fall king, book it in the next two weeks, and if you fish south of Pigeon Point, you have until Wednesday. Call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, the full moon. Last week we told you the good moon was coming. It is here. Saturday the moon sets with the sun coming up and rises as it goes down, and there is not a single permitted tournament on Pine Flat, Millerton, Kaweah or Success. If you have been waiting for a quiet ramp and a good almanac day at the same time, this is the weekend.

Third, the numbers we pulled back. We printed Central Coast lake levels last week that we now believe came from a frozen feed. We said so above and took them off the current list. When a source goes stale, we would rather tell you than let a number sit in the table looking fresh.
