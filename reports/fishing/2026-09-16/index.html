For two months we have told you the lower Kings was unfishable, and this week that changed in one line on a Corps sheet. Pine Flat’s release went from 1,690 cfs last Wednesday to 626 cfs this morning, a 63% cut in seven days. Irrigation demand is coming off. And in the same week, CDFW planted rainbows in the Kings below the dam in all three counties it runs through, Fresno, Kings and Tulare. Fresh fish and a flow you can finally work. That is the best inland news we have had to print since spring.

Offshore, the salmon bite came down off last week’s peak and then just… stayed. We reported 1.90 a rod last Tuesday. Since then the northern fleet has run 1.05, 0.96, 1.38 and 1.15, four straight days within striking distance of a fish a rod, which is a perfectly good salmon fishery and a lot more durable than a spike. Lovely Martha put 14 kings on seven rods Monday with a 35-pounder, the biggest fish we have seen reported this season. New Easy Rider went 24 on 12 Tuesday. Straight limits, twice in two days.

South of us the ocean has gone silly. San Diego landings put 940 yellowfin on the dock on Tuesday alone, plus 270 dorado. Pronto out of Oceanside went 120 yellowfin on twelve rods; Blackfish went 40 on four. Both of those are ten fish a rod on a day trip. Skipper Michael Potvin, running out of 22nd Street, called it “this 2026 El Niño” and said he sees it “getting better and better going into fall.” And from Bodega down to Avila, rockfish boats are hitting straight ten-fish limits, boat after boat, day after day.

Two cautions before you go. First, there are no minus tides at all this week at any of our four stations. If you were planning around drained water, plan again. Second, the Section 7 sheet blanked Don Pedro, McClure, Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence and the McSwain release for the second week running, and there are permitted tournaments on Don Pedro and McClure this Saturday. We print last week’s numbers for those, date-stamped and starred. We do not print them as current. Details in Water Watch.

◉ This Week’s Best Bets

 

Bet No. 1 · Fresno & Tulare

The Kings Below Pine Flat

The release dropped from 1,690 cfs to 626 in seven days, and CDFW planted rainbows below the dam in Fresno, Kings and Tulare counties the week of Sept. 13–19. Fresh fish, and for the first time since spring a flow a person can read. Nightcrawlers, salmon eggs and small spinners in the softer water behind structure. 626 cfs is still moving water; pick your crossings or stay on the bank.

Bet No. 2 · Golden Gate

Fall Kings, While They Last

Four straight days between 0.96 and 1.38 a rod, a 35-pounder on Lovely Martha Monday, and straight limits on New Easy Rider both Monday and Tuesday. 46 open days left north of Pigeon Point, 15 south of it, but both areas share one 20,000-Chinook guideline and either can close inseason. Book it and call the hotline the morning you go.

Bet No. 3 · San Diego to Newport

Yellowfin, Wide Open

940 yellowfin across San Diego landings on Tuesday alone, plus 270 dorado. Pronto out of Oceanside: 120 yellowfin on 12 rods. Up the coast at Newport, Blackfish out of Davey’s went 40 on four. Both exactly ten a rod on a day trip. Grande ran 4.24 a rod, Old Glory 5.00 overnight. Wahoo and striped marlin are showing on the longer boats. It is a long tow from the Valley; it is also the best tuna fishing in years.

Bet No. 4 · Morro Bay & Avila

Rockfish, Limits Every Trip

Thirteen separate trips we counted came in at exactly ten fish a rod, the legal aggregate. Patriot out of Avila did it twice, Starfire and Endeavor out of Morro did it three times between them, and Morro is stacking lingcod to 12 pounds on top. Closest reliable saltwater limits to the Valley, about three hours out of Fresno. Watch the sublimits: copper 1, canary 2, vermilion 4.

⚠ Water Watch

The Pine Flat release is the story. 1,690 cfs last Wednesday, 626 cfs this morning, down 63%. Storage barely moved as a result: the lake gave up just 491 acre-feet in seven days against 13,651 the week before. Pine Flat sits at 158,996 AF, 15.9% of gross pool, which is still very low, but it has stopped falling off a cliff. Below the dam, the Kings near Trimmer reads 211.5 cfs.

Isabella took the week’s biggest hit, off 6,456 acre-feet to 159,506, or 28.1%. Success gave up 1,334 AF and Kaweah 853. The Kaweah at Three Rivers is down to 37.5 cfs and the Kern at Kernville to 175.2 cfs. The Fresno River above Hensley reads a flat zero.

And the blanks are back, in the worst possible week. The Section 7 sheet published “No Project Data Available at this Time” for Don Pedro and McClure and “-NR-” for Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence and the flow below McSwain. There are permitted bass tournaments on Don Pedro Friday through Sunday and on McClure Saturday. Bite Report

A straight word about this table. The weekly regional aggregator filed “no reports for this week” for Central California, Central Sierra, Sierra Foothills and the Delta, and the most recent Pine Flat guide post on that service is from November 2024. So what follows is built on verified flows, storage, stocking records and September pattern, not on somebody else’s fresh fish story. Where we have no basis, we say so.

WaterWhat We Know
Pine Flat Stocked with Chinook in June and rainbows through the spring. At 15.9% the fish are concentrated and the thermocline is shallow. Kings and rainbows both follow the shad; troll deep off the main-lake points and bluff walls early and late. Bass have pulled off the flats, work ledges and brush. Ramp conditions are the risk, not the bite.
Millerton Rose on the week in percentage terms relative to its neighbors and is the healthiest Valley-floor reservoir on this page at 44.1%. Spotted bass on finesse, catfish off the bank after dark. Cools and improves from here as boat traffic drops.
Don Pedro & McClure No published level for the second week running. Both fisheries are shad-driven in September, trout and kings on the bait schools, bass deep. There are tournaments on both this weekend, so ramps will be busy and the operators will know the water. Call them.
New Melones Off 7,872 AF, at 64.4%, the largest absolute drawdown on the sheet, but off the biggest lake. Stanislaus natural inflow is down to 79 cfs and Goodwin is releasing 202. Trout and kokanee are moving toward spawn; bass are a grind.
Eastman & Hensley The two thinnest lakes we cover: 6.9% and 7.5%. The Fresno River above Hensley is at zero cfs. Eastman is the better bass water of the two by a wide margin; Hensley is catfish and carp. At these levels both are launch-first problems.
Kaweah & Success Kaweah 11.8%, Success 16.8%, and the Kaweah at Three Rivers is down to 37.5 cfs. Both fish small: finesse plastics for spotted and largemouth, minijigs for crappie, dip bait for cats after dark. Golden Empire Bass Club is on Success this Saturday.
Shaver No published storage this week. Last figure is 89,463 AF on Sept. 8, 66.1%. Mid-September is when kokanee stop being a target and head for the inlets, and the holdover rainbows follow them in for the eggs. That is pattern, not a report; nobody filed one.
Wishon & Courtright Neither appears on any Corps sheet, and we could not verify a level or a current report for either. Both are high-country trout waters that fish well into the fall. Call ahead; Courtright in particular has had access work in recent seasons and we have nothing current on it.

Mono County got the best plant list in the state this week. CDFW stocked rainbows in Big Virginia Lake, Little Virginia Lake, Convict Creek and Rock Creek sections 1 and 2 for the week of Sept. 13–19. That is the June Lake Loop corridor and the Rock Creek drainage, fresh fish in cold water at elevation, at exactly the time the crowds thin out and the aspens turn.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

This is the part of the state where “I checked the statewide rules” gets people a citation. Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river; the Owens above a bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules.

A handful of Mono County waters, including the East Walker, are open year-round, and during the off-season those waters are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only, no bait. Most of the rest of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We could not confirm the exact 2026 general-season closing date on CDFW’s own pages this morning, so we are not going to print one. If you are planning a late-season trip, open the current CDFW regulations booklet to the Sierra District pages and read the entry for the specific water, not the summary.

WaterConditions & What We Can Verify
The Delta No fresh regional column filed this week. What we can tell you is the calendar: nine permitted bass tournaments launch on Delta water this Saturday alone, across Contra Costa, Sacramento and San Joaquin counties, plus two-day events running into Sunday. Ramps at Ladd’s, Russo’s, B&W and Tracy Oasis will be stacked at first light. If you are a weekend angler who does not like company, go Friday or go Sunday afternoon. No minus tides this week to swing the striper bite; the Port Chicago range is modest all week and the lowest water of the cycle does not arrive until next Wednesday morning.
Upper Kings, above Pine Flat The gauge below the North Fork near Trimmer reads 211.5 cfs this morning: low, clear, wadeable freestone water. The Corps published no natural-flow figure for the Kings above the lake again this week, and Mill Creek near Piedra came back “not reported.” Fish it small and light and stay off the skyline.
Lower Kings, below the dam The headline of the issue. Release cut from 1,690 cfs to 626 in seven days, and CDFW planted rainbows in the reach across Fresno, Kings and Tulare counties the week of Sept. 13–19. The classic stretch is the roughly five miles from below the dam down toward Alta Weir. Nightcrawlers, salmon eggs and small spinners. Wading warning: 626 cfs is a great deal friendlier than 1,690 and it is still not a stroll. Water this cold pushes harder than it looks. Wear the vest, use a staff, and if you are alone, fish from the bank.
Kaweah & Tule The Kaweah at Three Rivers is at 37.5 cfs, skinny. Every Tule River gauge on the Corps sheet came back “not reported” this morning: South Fork near Success, near Springville, and Deer Creek at Trenton Weir. We have nothing verified on the Tule this week.
Kern River At Kernville the river is down to 175.2 cfs. CDFW planted Kern River sections 5 and 6 in Tulare County this week. Low, clear and freshly stocked, a good weekend if you want moving water without a tow.
California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay We have no verified current report for the aqueduct system, San Luis or the forebay this week; no regional column was filed and these are not on the Corps sheets we pull. September pattern is catfish on cut bait and dip bait along the canal, with stripers around the check gates early. The standing warning does not depend on a report: aqueduct water is swift, cold, steep-sided and has no way out. Wear a PFD, fish with somebody, and stay off wet concrete.

Catalina & the Island Boats

One skipper named the island in his own words this week. Michael Potvin, running the Pride out of 22nd Street Landing in San Pedro, filed this on Sept. 13 off a 1.5-day charter:

“The weather conditions were not in our favor. We started at the island for a handful of yellowtail, Bonita, excellent sheepshead fishing and bags full of taco meat; we ran away from the weather and went offshore in search of kelps and managed a handful Dorado and some wide open mini yellowfin fishing.”

His count for the whole trip: 48 yellowfin, 6 dorado, 5 yellowtail, 28 sheephead, 31 calico bass, 29 bonito, 64 rockfish, 2 bullet tuna. Read that split the way he wrote it: the island produced the yellowtail, sheephead, bonito, calico and rockfish. The tuna and the dorado came after they left the island and ran offshore. We are not going to credit Catalina with 48 yellowfin it did not produce.

The next night Potvin came in from another 1.5-day with a wahoo, 120 yellowfin, 9 dorado and three marlin released, and wrote: “you wanna get in on this 2026 El Niño, I see this Fishing getting better and better going into fall!!!” That trip he did not place at the island, so we are not placing it there either.

If you want a boat that is actually going to the island: 22nd Street Landing has the Pursuit scheduled on Full Day Limited Load Catalina Island Freelance trips on Sept. 17, 21, 22, 23 and 24, (310) 832-8304. The Pursuit’s recent full-day counts are strong on the mixed-bag species the island produces: 18 yellowtail, 33 calico and 130 bonito on 38 anglers Tuesday; 17 yellowtail, 31 calico and 141 bonito on 39 Monday. Fair warning: the count service does not break those out by location, so we can tell you what the landing schedules and what the boat caught, but not that every one of those fish came off Catalina. Davey’s Locker’s count page was checked as well and attributed no trip in this window to the island.

⏳ The Season Clock

Salmon: 46 days north of Pigeon Point. 15 days south.

38°02′ N (near Point Reyes) to Pigeon Point: open Sept. 1 – Oct. 31. Counting today, 46 open days left.
Pigeon Point to the U.S./Mexico border: open Sept. 1 – 30. Counting today, 15 open days left.

Both areas share a single 20,000-Chinook fall harvest guideline, and CDFW states plainly that inseason action may close open days when harvest approaches it. Two salmon a day, any species except coho, 20-inch minimum. No filleting on the boat. North of Point Conception: two single-point single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler.

Here is the honest part. We checked the CDFW Sport Ocean Salmon Tracker this morning. It shows the full 20,000 still remaining, but it is stamped “Data updated through: 8/30/2026,” which is two days before the fall season opened. Catch estimates post by half-month period and the Sept. 1–15 numbers are not out yet. So nobody outside CDFW currently knows how much of the fall guideline is gone. Given what the fleet has been landing, assume more than zero.

Call the hotline the morning you go: (707) 576-3429. Last month federal inseason action shut San Francisco a week early on a Thursday announcement. The same mechanism is live right now.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

 

Phase computed from the synodic month (29.530588853 days) against the reference new moon of Jan. 6, 2000, 18:14 UTC. Sunrise and sunset computed for Fresno (36.75°N, 119.77°W), PDT. By phase alone this is the weakest week of the month, and we are going to say so. The new moon was Friday the 11th; that bracket is behind us. The solunar timing says something different, and it is worth hearing. We are three days off the equinox, so sunrise and sunset sit almost exactly twelve hours apart, and on Friday the 18th and Saturday the 19th the two majors land right on top of them. You very rarely get both majors bracketing both light changes on the same day. Phase says middling. Timing says fish Friday and Saturday, first light and last.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

A lower flow on the Kings is good news and a new hazard at the same time. 626 cfs looks walkable from the bank in a way 1,690 never did, and that is exactly the problem. It is still cold, pushy tailwater on a slick cobble bottom, and there are fresh planted trout in it giving people a reason to take one more step. Wear the vest. Use a staff. If you are by yourself, fish from the bank. More people drown in water they thought was fine than in water that scared them.

Pine Flat is at 15.9% and Kaweah is at 11.8%. The storage has stabilized but the water is still down where it was, and every rock, stump and old roadbed that cleared your hull in July is at prop depth now. Throw out every waypoint you set before this summer. Idle in, watch the sounder, and expect at least one ramp to end in dirt.

Seven projects on this page have no published level, and two of them have tournaments this Saturday. Don Pedro, McClure, Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence and the McSwain release all came back empty for the second straight week. A blank row is not a safe row; it means nobody has said where the water is. If you are towing to Don Pedro or McClure this weekend, call the marina or the operating agency first. Every time, not just this time.

No minus tides is its own kind of trap. Last week the hazard was people out on drained reef in the dark. This week it is the opposite mistake: anglers who fished a rock or a bar a fortnight ago at a minus low going back to it now and finding two or three more feet of water over it, moving differently. Rocky shorelines, jetties and surf perch beaches all fish differently on a flat tide. Check the table above for your station before you commit, and never turn your back on the ocean.

Fire danger stays extreme in the high country. If you are headed to Shaver, Huntington, Edison, Florence, Mammoth Pool, Wishon, Courtright or over the hill to the June Lake Loop and Rock Creek, check road and fire status before you leave and know your second way out. One closed road is a far bigger problem at 8,000 feet than it is on the valley floor.

And the heat. The equinox lands Tuesday and this valley has not read the calendar. Off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening, take more water than you think you need, take shade and a hat, and tell somebody where you went and when you will be back.

Two things to act on this week, and for once they do not pull in the same direction. First, the lower Kings. We have been telling you to stay out of it since June. The release is down 63% in a week and CDFW has put fresh rainbows in it across three counties. If you have been waiting for a reason to fish close to home, this is it, and it may not last: the release schedule is somebody else’s decision and it can go back up as fast as it came down.

Second, the salmon clock. The bite has settled into something sustainable, around a fish a rod, four days running, with a 35-pounder in there. The calendar says 46 days north of Pigeon Point and 15 south. But the guideline is 20,000 fish shared between both areas, the last published catch estimate predates the season opener, and federal inseason action shut San Francisco a week early last month on a Thursday announcement. Do not plan on October. If you want a fall king, book one in the next two or three weeks and call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

And if you only have one day: take Friday. Both solunar majors sit right on sunrise and sunset, it is the day before twenty-odd tournaments hit the water, and the ramps will still be yours.
