Salmon is open, and the calendar is on our side. From Pigeon Point up to Point Reyes the season runs through Oct. 31, which is 25 days counting today. The stretch south of Pigeon Point closed last Wednesday, as scheduled. CDFW’s in-season changes page still lists Aug. 21 as its newest salmon action, with no change to the fall season. We read CDFW’s catch tracker directly this morning, and it still shows data only through Sept. 15: 7,585 caught, 12,415 left on the 20,000-fish guideline. The Sept. 16–30 estimate hasn’t been posted, so nobody outside CDFW knows the current number.

The boats are still finding kings. The northern fleet’s four-day tally, Saturday through Tuesday, is 203 kings on 146 rods, about 1.39 a rod. Tuesday alone was 70 on 47. New Easy Rider went 18 on 9 two days running, and California Dawn’s own post Tuesday read “Kings up to 26 pounds.” Those are the boats the count service listed. It may not list them all.

There are bluefin off the Bay. Monday Sorta Salty out of Bodega Bay came home with 10 bluefin to 65 pounds on five rods, two apiece. Scallywag out of Emeryville listed 6 on four rods Sunday, and Lady K listed 7 on six Tuesday. Down in San Diego, Pacific Queen’s three-day trip Sunday listed fish to 220 pounds. Dock totals are in the Ocean section.

New moon Saturday, and the tide lines up for it. The moon is new at about 8:50 a.m. Saturday, Oct. 10 by its true position, and the almanac puts the best fishing of the month around it. At the Golden Gate the evening low drops below zero three nights running: −0.06 ft at 6:43 p.m. Sunday, −0.11 at 7:21 p.m. Monday and −0.05 at 8:01 p.m. Tuesday. And Pine Flat is up 6,617 acre-feet on the week, to 16.4% full, after losing 555 the week before.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat gained ground. The Corps sheet (data through Tuesday midnight) has the lake at 164,123 AF, 16.4% full, up 6,617 AF since last Tuesday and up 1,312 on the day. The release averaged 249 cfs Tuesday, down from the 355 we reported a week ago. The Corps’ hourly report shows the release held at 249 cfs for the last 48 hours and the lake rising steadily, about 1,300 AF a day. That works out to roughly 900 cfs coming in, yet the gauge on the Kings below the North Fork, at the head of the lake, reads only 184 cfs. We can’t reconcile those two numbers, and the Corps itself warns its computed inflow is erratic. The storage gain itself is consistent hour after hour.

The south Valley lakes keep sliding. Isabella lost 3,948 AF on the week, to 25.8%, releasing an average 483 cfs against 241 coming in. Success lost 1,476, to 11.3%, and Kaweah 610, to 10.6%. Eastman (6.8%) and Hensley (7.4%) barely moved because there’s almost nothing left to move.

The foothill lakes are all dropping. The Corps’ Section 7 sheet carries current numbers for New Melones, Millerton and Bass Lake but nothing for Don Pedro, McClure or the Big Creek lakes, so we went to the state’s California Data Exchange Center (CDEC) for those. Through Monday: Don Pedro 74.9%, down 11,460 AF in a week; McClure 52.7%, down 12,140; Shaver 56.2%, down 5,222. Where CDEC and the Corps both report a lake (Millerton, New Melones), they match to the acre-foot. Golden Empire has Pine Flat Saturday, Kerman has Bass Lake, and Kings VIII has McClure Sunday. Expect trailers at all three.

⚠ The Regs Up Here Are Not the Regs Down Here

Inyo and Mono waters carry special regulations that change reach by reach on the same river. The Owens above a given bridge is not the Owens below it, and Hot Creek, the East Walker and the Upper Owens each have their own gear and take rules. Checking the statewide rules doesn’t cover you here.

A handful of Mono County waters, the East Walker among them, stay open year-round, and in the off-season those are catch-and-release, barbless, artificial lure or fly only. Most of the district runs on the general season, which closes in mid-November.

We still haven’t confirmed the exact 2026 closing date on CDFW’s own pages, so we’re not printing one. If you’re planning a late-season trip, read the Sierra District entry for your water in the current regulations booklet.

The count service doesn’t say where those fish came from, and we won’t guess. If you’re planning an island trip, ask whoever you book with where their boats have been fishing.

Rockfish rules changed Oct. 1 for every SoCal boat, island trips included. From Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, south of Point Conception, nearshore rockfish, cabezon and greenling are closed to boat anglers, and shelf rockfish and lingcod may only be taken seaward of the 50-fathom line. Shore anglers and divers are exempt. Yellowtail, calico, bonito and tuna aren’t affected. Also in force, per CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice: yellowfin 10 a day within the 20-fish general limit, bluefin 2 on top of it, and the emergency rule from Pigeon Point south: no hooks over 1½ inches and no wire leaders for shore anglers or anyone within 1,000 yards of shore. We re-checked all of this against CDFW’s groundfish summary this morning.

Two salmon a day, any species except coho, 20-inch minimum. No filleting on the boat. North of Point Conception: two single-point, single-shank barbless hooks, one rod per angler.

Call the hotline the morning you go: (707) 576-3429. Inseason closures can come with only a few days’ notice. That’s what happened to San Francisco in August.

Groundfish Limits

10 rockfish, cabezon and greenling per day, aggregate. Within that: copper 1, canary 2, and vermilion/sunset 2. The vermilion limit is 4 only in the Northern Management Area, north of Cape Mendocino (re-checked this morning against CDFW’s groundfish summary, updated June 23, 2026). Lingcod 2 a day, 22-inch minimum. No retention at any time of quillback, cowcod, yelloweye or bronzespotted.

Depths: Northern, Mendocino, San Francisco and Central areas (everything north of Point Conception) are open at all depths through Dec. 31. Southern area: nearshore species closed; shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of 50 fathoms only. You must carry a descending device. Cordell Bank: in June, federal managers replaced the old overlapping closures with a single Groundfish Exclusion Area around the core of the bank. All groundfish fishing is prohibited inside it. Check the boundary on CDFW’s Ocean Sport Fishing interactive map before you go.

◉ Moon Phase & Best Days

 

Phase computed from the synodic month (29.530588853 days) against the reference new moon of Jan. 6, 2000, 18:14 UTC, and checked against the moon’s true position. Sunrise, sunset, moonrise, moonset and lunar transits computed for Fresno (36.75°N, 119.77°W), PDT. Solunar majors are the moon overhead and underfoot, listed in the order they fall in the day. Illumination is at local noon. Almanac convention: the best fishing brackets the new and the full moon.

This is the good week. The moon is new at about 8:50 a.m. Saturday, Oct. 10 by its true position (about 2:14 a.m. Sunday by the mean synodic month). The almanac puts the best fishing around the new and full moons, so every day of this issue sits inside the new-moon bracket, with Friday through Sunday at its center. The next bracket, the full moon, is Oct. 25.

The clock is different at a new moon. The moon rises with the sun and sets with it, so there is no solunar major at dawn all week. The majors fall near midday and midnight (Saturday’s is 12:47 p.m.). What lands on first and last light are the minors, moonrise and moonset: Saturday’s moonrise is 7:07 a.m. against a 7:02 sunrise, and Sunday’s moonset is 6:46 p.m. against a 6:27 sunset. Sunday’s Golden Gate low, −0.06 ft at 6:43 p.m., lands within three minutes of moonset. That is the tide-and-moon overlap of the window, and it comes at dusk.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety

Low water on the Kings still makes people bold. The release has held at 249 cfs for two days, so you’ll see crossings and bars you couldn’t reach a couple of weeks ago, and a fourth week of planters on the other side. It’s still cold tailwater over slick cobble, and the release is set by request, so it can go back up. Wear the vest, carry a staff, and check the flow before you cross back.

Don’t eat shellfish from Monterey County. The state health department warned Sept. 14 against eating sport-harvested mussels, clams, scallops or oysters from Monterey County because of domoic acid, and the statewide sport mussel quarantine runs through at least Oct. 31. Anchovy taken from Pigeon Point to Point Lobos is bait only, by CDFW order since Sept. 11. Shellfish line: (800) 553-4133.

The minus tides come at dusk, and the Bay is busy this weekend. The Golden Gate low is −0.06 ft at 6:43 p.m. Sunday, −0.11 at 7:21 p.m. Monday and −0.05 at 8:01 p.m. Tuesday. If you walk a drained flat or reef, carry a real light and start back before the turn. Fleet Week’s Blue Angels fly Friday through Sunday, so expect heavy boat traffic and airshow restrictions on the water.

Young white sharks are still close to shore. The emergency rule from Pigeon Point south, confirmed in CDFW’s Sept. 17 notice, bans big hooks and wire leaders from shore and within 1,000 yards of it. If you hook one, cut the line. Don’t try to land it.

Low lakes, and a blank row isn’t a safe row. Pine Flat 16.4%, Success 11.3%, Kaweah 10.6%, Eastman 6.8%, and Don Pedro, McClure and Shaver each lost thousands of acre-feet this week. Every stump and roadbed that cleared your hull in July is closer to prop depth now. Idle in, watch the sounder, and call the marina before you tow.

Wind and fire season aren’t over. Check road, fire and wind before you head for Shaver, Huntington, the Kaiser Pass lakes, Wishon, or over the hill to the Owens, and know a second way out. Tell somebody where you went and when you’ll be back.

Three things this week. First, salmon. Pigeon Point to Point Reyes is open through Oct. 31, 25 days counting today, and the Bay boats put 203 kings on 146 rods over the last four days. CDFW’s own tracker still stops at Sept. 15, so nobody outside CDFW knows how many fish are left, and we won’t guess. If you want a king, go soon, and call (707) 576-3429 the morning you go.

Second, Saturday’s new moon. It’s the almanac’s best bracket of the month, and the Golden Gate’s evening lows go below zero Sunday, Monday and Tuesday right at dusk. Bring a light if you’re walking a flat, and know when to turn back.

Third, the lakes. Pine Flat is up 6,617 AF and still only 16.4% full, and Don Pedro, McClure and Shaver all dropped hard this week. Call the marina before you tow.
