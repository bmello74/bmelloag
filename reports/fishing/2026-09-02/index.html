The fall salmon season opened Tuesday and the fleet emptied the ocean into the fish boxes. Twenty-two boats posted salmon trips between Bodega Bay and San Jose. Eighteen of them came in at exactly two a rod — full limits. Add it up and it is 540 kings on 282 anglers, 1.9 a rod, which is ninety-six percent of a perfect limit day across an entire fleet. We have not printed a number like that all year.

The grade was there too. Surf Scooter out of Bodega had a 33-pounder, Lovely Martha out of San Francisco a 32, Outer Limits out of Sausalito a 30. Jared Davis on the Salty Lady posted the line of the day: “But I thought Salmon season was over.” Fish Emeryville called it “an absolutely lights out day for the fleet.”

One consequence worth naming: the northern fleet stopped fishing the bottom. Last week we led with rockfish going sixteen for seventeen at limits. On Tuesday, out of twenty-two northern trips, we count two rockfish and four lingcod, total, all of them incidental to a salmon trip. The boats did not lose the rockfish bite. They left it. If you want a sack instead of a king, you will have most of that reef to yourself for a few weeks.

Inland, two rivers reversed and one collapsed. The upper Kings and the Kern at Kernville both came up this week after weeks of falling — the first good news that section has had since July. Meanwhile the Goodwin release dropped from 1,388 cfs to 202, a seven-fold cut in a week on the lower Stanislaus. And Pine Flat is under eighteen percent. Details in Water Watch.

The Kern and the Upper Kings Turned

First reversal we have printed in that column since July. Kings natural flow into Pine Flat is back to 293.3 cfs from 185.5 — up 58% in seven days. The Kern at Kernville is 189.3, up from 176.7. ● No. 4 — Moon & Tide

Fish the Back Half This Time

Exact opposite of last week. The moon goes dark toward a new moon on September 11, and the minus tides arrive Sunday and deepen every day. The biggest drain of the window is Labor Day night into Tuesday morning — 6.36 ft down to −0.51 at the Gate, a 6.9-foot fall. Wait for it.

⚠ Water Watch

Pine Flat: another 5.6 feet gone, and it is now under eighteen percent. From 754.08 ft and 186,532 acre-feet a week ago to 748.45 ft and 173,138 acre-feet at midnight Tuesday. That is 13,394 acre-feet in seven days and it puts the lake at 17.3% of gross pool, down from 18.7% last week and 20.2% three weeks ago. The drawdown has now taken twenty-two feet off this lake in twenty-two days.

Two rivers reversed, and we want to be careful about how we say it. Kings natural flow above the lake is 293.3 cfs against 185.5 last week — up 58%. The Kern at Kernville is 189.3 against 176.7, its first rise in three weeks. Kaweah at Three Rivers is 46.0, up from 40.8. Both Tule gages held. These are real and they are measured, but a one-week rise in early September is not a trend — it is usually an operations change upstream, not weather. Enjoy it, do not plan a month around it.

The Goodwin release collapsed. 1,388 cfs last week, 202 cfs this morning. That is a seven-fold cut on the lower Stanislaus in seven days, and it is the single biggest change on this page. Last week we told you the jump up was what catches waders out. The drop back down is the same problem in reverse — different channel, different holes, different wading. Look at it before you step in, and understand it can go back the other way just as fast.

Pine Flat’s own release is easing. 1,742 cfs, down from 1,884 last week and 2,174 the week before. Over six weeks that line reads 4,226, 3,071, 2,003, 2,174, 1,884, 1,742. It is grinding down. It is still not a wading flow. Bank or boat below the dam.

The Section 7 sheet is thin again, but we found part of it elsewhere. Don Pedro and New Exchequer both came back “No Project Data Available at this Time” for the second straight week, and Shaver, Huntington, Florence, Edison and flow below McSwain are all “-NR-”. This week we went to the state’s CDEC database and recovered Don Pedro, McClure and Shaver at midnight August 31 — two days old, from a different agency, and marked as such in the table. Huntington, Florence, Edison and McSwain we still do not have.

And one small loss. Bear Creek at McKee Road fell to 113.1 cfs from 190.4, and Kaweah at McKay Point slipped to 13.0 from 14.0. The Fresno River above Hensley is zero for the sixth straight week. Mill Creek near Piedra is dry.

⚠ Before You Drive Over — Different Rulebook

Special regulations are the norm over there, not the exception. Individual Eastern Sierra waters carry their own gear restrictions, size limits, reduced bag limits and seasonal closures. Several run barbless-only or catch-and-release stretches, and Crowley in particular fishes under tighter rules than the statewide standard — restricted baits and a reduced limit with a size minimum, and the rules change with the calendar. Look up the specific water in the current CDFW freshwater regulations before you launch. Do not assume the five-fish valley rulebook travels with you.

Getting there from the valley: Tioga Pass (Highway 120) is the direct route to the June Lake Loop and it is a seasonal road — open now, but check Caltrans before an early start. Figure roughly five hours from Fresno to June Lake, six to Bridgeport. Sonora Pass (108) and Monitor Pass (89) are the northern alternatives.

Labor Day is Monday, September 7 — inside this window. Bridgeport, Twin Lakes and the June Loop fill to the edges, campgrounds go first-come-full, and 395 backs up both directions. With no fresh plants over there this week, we would rather send you Tuesday the 8th than Saturday the 5th.

◉ Delta, Rivers & Aqueduct

The Delta — Fish the Tide, Because That Is What We Can Measure

Honest accounting, second week running. The weekly aggregator we track filed “No reports for this week” under Delta. The fall striped bass run is still on — nothing has happened to reverse it and it normally builds right through September — but no new guide report was filed inside this window, so what follows is the standing pattern plus this week’s measured tide. We are not putting fresh dates on old quotes.

The tide is the actionable part, and this week it runs backwards from last week. Port Chicago starts soft and finishes hard. Today’s low is 0.8 ft at 12:16 a.m. and the daily swing is modest. Then the lows fall every single day — 0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.3, 0.1 — and on Tuesday the 8th Port Chicago goes minus for the first time in weeks, −0.1 ft at 7:12 a.m., and again Wednesday at 7:53 a.m. Pair that with the 4.3 ft high at 1:20 p.m. Tuesday and you have the strongest moving water in the west Delta of the whole window, on the two days after the holiday.

The general rule stands: two hours before the high into the peak, blood worms, pile worms or fresh shad, with the better fish on shad. For largemouth, the early-September pattern is wood and moving water — flip and punch the tule edges where there is current, big profiles on the high tide, reaction baits when it blows. Small bass dominate the numbers. If the wind lays flat, prop baits with long pauses.

Sturgeon status, unchanged and still not law. The Fish and Game Commission voted 3–2 on August 15 to allow catch-and-release only for white sturgeon, with no harvest. As of this morning we have seen nothing confirming the Office of Administrative Law has acted, and no opening date has been published. Three weeks on, that is still the whole story. Do not put a card in your pocket on the strength of this paragraph. Confirm with CDFW that the regulation is actually in effect before you fish.

California Aqueduct, San Luis & O’Neill Forebay

San Luis and O’Neill are Reclamation and DWR facilities and appear on neither Corps sheet, so we still have no independently measured storage here — that has been true every week we have published. The standing pattern: bank stripers around Dinosaur Point on jumbo minnows, anchovies or blood worms; the forebay slowed by thick grass limiting access; trollers grinding between wind windows for a handful of fish and the occasional very large one.

Two things to know before you go. Boat inspections are required on exit at San Luis, O’Neill Forebay and Los Banos Creek — golden mussels were detected at the forebay, and boats leaving will not get a clean-boat tag. And in the south aqueduct in Kern County, cooler water has kept striper action honest on cut sardines, anchovies or jumbo minnows, with catfish steady on dip bait in the calm water below check gates.

The state publishes a map of the 16 designated aqueduct fishing locations through the Department of Water Resources recreation pages. Worth having on your phone — access is not legal everywhere along that ditch.

⚠ Heat & Water Safety — Labor Day Weekend

Pine Flat is at 17.3% and has dropped twenty-two feet in twenty-two days. That is still the most dangerous number in this issue, and there is a two-day tournament on it this weekend. Rock, stump and old roadbed that were well under your hull a month ago are at prop depth now, and the ramps are ending in dirt. Idle in, watch the sounder, and throw out every waypoint you set before August.

The Goodwin release fell from 1,388 cfs to 202 in one week. A drop is not automatically safer than a rise — it moves the channel, exposes gravel that was swimmable last weekend, and strands boats that launched on the old flow. And it can go back up just as fast as it came down. Look at the lower Stanislaus with your own eyes before you commit.

The lower Kings is at 1,742 cfs. Easing for six straight weeks and still not a wading flow. Bank or boat below the dam, and wear the vest.

Labor Day is the busiest water weekend of the fall. Every ramp on this page will be full, the Kaiser Pass road is one lane in places, and Bass Lake and the June Loop will be at capacity. More boats means more wakes, more inexperienced operators and slower emergency response. If your trip can move to Tuesday the 8th, move it — that is also the best tide of the window.

Fire danger is extreme in the high country. That is the ranger district’s wording, not ours. If you are headed to Edison, Florence, Mammoth Pool, Wishon, Courtright or Portal Forebay, check road and fire status before you leave and know your second way out. Holiday traffic makes a single closed road a much bigger problem.

Heat, and the water itself. Valley afternoons are still the real risk on this list — off the water by mid-morning or fish the evening, and take water, shade, a hat and somebody who knows where you went. On the coast, those deep minus tides Monday through Wednesday put people out on rock and reef that the ocean takes back fast. Watch the flood, and never turn your back on it.

Two things to act on. First: if you want a king off a party boat, book it now. The fall season opened with eighteen of twenty-two boats at full limits, and the entire fall allocation is 20,000 fish shared between the two open areas. A day like Tuesday burns roughly three percent of it. The calendar says October 31 for the Point Reyes-to-Pigeon Point water and September 30 south of there. The arithmetic says do not count on either.

Second, and this is the opposite of what we told you last week: wait. The moon goes dark toward a new moon on the 11th and the minus tides deepen every day from Sunday on. The best water of these eight days is Tuesday the 8th and Wednesday the 9th — after the holiday, after the crowds go home, on the widest tidal swing of the window. If you have a day to spend, spend it then.

Tight lines,
