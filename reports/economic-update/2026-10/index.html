📈 Dairy Economic Outlook

USDA blinked on milk supply. After raising production two months running, the October 9 WASDE cut 2026 output 300 million lbs to 236.9 billion lbs and 2027 to 238.4 billion. The 2026 all-milk price held at $19.90/cwt; Class III was trimmed 40¢ to $15.80 and Class IV raised 35¢ to $18.90.

2027 just got better, and different. USDA raised 2027 all-milk 65¢ to $20.45, but last month’s flip is gone: Class IV at $18.90 now tops Class III at $16.90 next year too. Futures agree, with November Class IV at $20.90 against Class III at $16.14 (Oct 8 settles).

California: Dairy Market News reports milk beginning to tighten here, with spot milk requests emerging and hauling costs still high.

🌾 Feed Relief & The Depooling Watch

Corn gave some back. December corn at $4.82¾ is down roughly 50¢ from our September issue after USDA raised the yield to 181.2 bu/ac. If you buy corn forward, this is a better window than you had a month ago. Meal is not cooperating: December soybean meal settled $368.70/ton on Oct 12.

Hay is firm. Central San Joaquin Valley Premium/Supreme alfalfa traded $270/ton FOB and $353 delivered to the dairy (USDA AMS, week ending Oct 9), with demand described as good.

The spread is the story. At $2.90, handlers have an incentive to pull Class IV milk off the pool, and that moves your blend price and PPD against you. Read the PPD line, not the announced class price.

Diesel has backed off its peak, but not by much. Two pieces of relief are in play, a G7 strategic reserve release weighted toward diesel and a federal diesel excise deferral, but neither has shown up at the pump in a big way yet.

⚠ What’s Driving This

The supply picture has improved. The diesel picture has not caught up yet:

• Gulf oil is moving again. Flows are near prewar levels, with about 40% routed around the Strait of Hormuz, which still has not formally reopened.
• Tanker attacks are rising, roughly one a day since Oct 2, and Houthi strikes on Riyadh have widened the conflict.
• Distillate is the tight spot. East Coast stocks ran 32% below the five-year average in September.
• Relief measures: the G7 agreed Oct 2 to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel over four months, on top of a 40-million-barrel U.S. SPR exchange.
• California on top of that: the Valero Benicia and Phillips 66 Los Angeles closures took out about 17% of state refining capacity. The state allowed winter-blend gasoline early on Sep 28.

📊 30-Day Fuel Price Outlook

EIA’s October Short-Term Energy Outlook has U.S. diesel averaging $5.81 in Q4 and $4.92 in Q1 2027, with Brent at $104.69 this quarter and easing toward $84 for 2027. California runs well above the national number. Plan on $8.10–$8.50/gal clear and roughly $7.20–$7.60 red through mid-November.

On the federal excise deferral: an Oct 5 executive order defers the 24.4¢ federal diesel tax through Dec 31 and relaxes dyed-fuel enforcement on highways. Ask your supplier how it is being passed through before you change anything about how you fuel.

Action: If your supplier will write a volume contract near current levels for winter needs, take a hard look. The 2027 crude curve is still pricing $84–$90, not a return to pre-war prices.
Crude Oil Context. Brent settled $105.69/barrel and WTI $94.14 on October 13, Brent roughly flat and WTI about $8 lower than in our September issue. Crude is still up more than 70% this year. Saudi Aramco says about 3 billion barrels of supply has been lost since February and inventories may take up to two years to rebuild, which is why diesel is slow to come down even as flows recover. Every $10/barrel move is worth about 24¢/gal at the pump.

🌰 2026 Nut Harvest Watch

Almonds: strong shipping, heavy inventory. The September position report (released Oct 13) showed shipments of 232.5 million lbs, up 17.9%, with exports up 28% to 189.2 million lbs and domestic shipments down 12.4%. Receipts to date are 1.216 billion lbs, up 22.5%. The number to watch is uncommitted inventory at 710 million lbs, up 28.5%, while commitments slipped 3.6%. The industry is working off a 2.7 billion lb estimate. That much unsold product keeps a lid on price even with exports running hot.

Walnuts are the opposite story: USDA forecasts 680,000 tons, down 16% from 809,000, after nut set fell 18%, and the industry entered the season short on inventory. Pistachios are finishing a light off-year crop after spring heat hurt pollination in the southern Valley.

What that means for you: almond price will be decided by how fast that inventory moves, not by what you do this fall, so put your effort where you control the outcome: post-harvest irrigation, soil building, and a clean start to the 2027 crop.

October 19, 1781, The Surrender at Yorktown

Four days from now, on October 19, marks 245 years since General Charles Cornwallis surrendered his army at Yorktown, Virginia. Washington’s Continental Army and its French allies had marched south from New York, dug siege lines around the town, and pounded it for three weeks while the French fleet held the Chesapeake shut. There was no way out by land or by sea.

The men in those trenches were not professional soldiers by trade. Many were farmers who had already lost harvests to the war, serving long past the enlistments they had signed, often unpaid and short of shoes. They stayed because the outcome mattered more than the season.

Yorktown did not end the war on paper; the treaty took two more years. But it decided it. Everything that came after, the right to own the ground you farm and keep what it produces, runs back to a muddy siege line in a Virginia October. It is worth the minute.

“

I have the Honor to inform Congress, that a Reduction of the British Army under the Command of Lord Cornwallis, is most happily effected.

George Washington
Letter to the President of Congress, October 19, 1781
