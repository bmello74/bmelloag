📈  This Week’s Call

Wholesale diesel keeps falling and the pump is not following. We called a third straight decline, and we were wrong.

Last Monday we called a third straight weekly decline in California diesel and a plateau in gasoline. We got diesel wrong. AAA’s Sunday read puts California diesel at $8.3910, up 1.8 cents on the week and 4.8 cents under the $8.4387 record set September 22. Our range was $8.20 to $8.37, so the actual landed 2.1 cents over the top. EIA’s survey for the week of October 5 printed $8.082, down 9.9 cents, against a national average of $6.199, down 18.3 cents, and came in 3.8 cents under the bottom of our EIA range. The wholesale half of the call held. Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel spot fell another 16.6 cents to $4.870 for the week ending October 2, after falling 32.9 cents the week before. The rebound of more than 20 cents we said would prove us wrong did not come. The third distillate draw we flagged did arrive, but at 0.1 million barrels it is not the signal we meant. Gasoline eased more than we allowed. Regular finished at $6.3069, down 8.2 cents and 13.1 cents from the $6.4375 record, 2.3 cents under the bottom of our range. Crude edged up. WTI settled Friday at $91.85, up 0.8% on the week, and Brent at $104.72, up 2.4%, as Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida and operators shut in roughly 70% of U.S. Gulf oil output. Trump said the U.S. will not strike Iran before the November 3 midterms. Our read: retail diesel is sitting 4.8 cents from a record on top of a wholesale market that has dropped 49.5 cents in two weeks. That gap has to close, and with diesel stocks at a record seasonal low and Gulf refiners in a hurricane’s path, we now lean flat to higher, not lower.

Last Week at the Pump

AAA’s Sunday, October 11 read puts California diesel at $8.3910, up 1.8 cents on the week and 4.8 cents under the September 22 record. Regular finished at $6.3069, down 8.2 cents. Diesel is up 41 cents in a month and roughly $3.24 in a year; gasoline is up 38 cents in a month and $1.64 in a year. EIA’s survey for the week of Monday, October 5 put California on-highway diesel at $8.082, down 9.9 cents, against a national average of $6.199, down 18.3 cents; EIA regular was $6.227, up 3.8 cents. Here is the number that matters: the Valley split five up and four down, and no metro moved more than about six cents. Hanford gave back 6 cents of last week’s 14-cent drop, Visalia fell about 6, and Bakersfield is now the cheapest fill we track.

Diesel detail: Off-road dyed diesel is exempt from California’s 48.2¢ state excise and the 24.4¢ federal excise, roughly a 72.6¢ per gallon spread before sales tax. At Fresno’s $8.458 pump that puts dyed product near $7.73 a gallon, about $7,730 to fill a 1,000-gallon on-farm tank, essentially unchanged from last week. Price your rack and cardlock against these numbers, not the sign on the highway. Here is the test for your delivered price: Los Angeles wholesale diesel has fallen 49.5 cents in two weeks and the statewide pump price is 4.8 cents from a record. If your delivered price has not come down, ask your supplier where the difference went. Wholesale leads the pump by one to two weeks, so some of the gap is timing. Not all of it should be.

For your surcharge tables. The DOE national on-highway diesel average of $6.199 for the week of October 5 governs fuel surcharges through the middle of the month. It fell 18.3 cents, almost twice California’s 9.9-cent drop, so the national index is running against you again. California now runs $1.88 a gallon above the national number, up from $1.80. The fix has not changed: index to the EIA California series or a West Coast rack, get it in writing, and stop letting a Gulf Coast number price a Central Valley load.

The Week Ahead: Our Forecast

For the week of October 12 through 18. WTI settled Friday at $91.85, up 36 cents on the day and up 0.8% on the week. Brent finished at $104.72, up 2.4% on the week. Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel spot for the week ending October 2 came in at $4.870, down 16.6 cents, and Los Angeles gasoline spot rose 14.4 cents to $4.438. Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida late Friday and shut in roughly 70% of U.S. Gulf oil output. Retail diesel is stuck near its record. Wholesale says it should fall and the storm says it might not.

▲ Pushing Prices Up

Hurricane Isaias hit the Gulf. It made landfall near Destin, Florida late Friday and operators shut in roughly 70% of U.S. Gulf oil output. Chevron’s 356,000-barrel-a-day Pascagoula refinery and Vertex’s Mobile refinery, about 2.4% of U.S. capacity, sit in or near the track.

Diesel stocks are at a record seasonal low. Analyst Andrew Lipow says U.S. diesel stocks are the lowest for this time of year since EIA began collecting data in 1982. EIA’s weekly table shows 105.1 million barrels, down another 0.1 million.

Hormuz is still the wild card. Iranian media reported “massive explosions” in the southern Strait Friday and said tankers on unauthorized routes may have hit sea mines. Iran is reviewing the U.S. response to its Hormuz plan and says it will reply in “the next few days.”

▼ Holding Prices Down

Wholesale keeps falling. Los Angeles diesel spot dropped 16.6 cents to $4.870 for the week ending October 2, 49.5 cents in two prints. Retail has not caught down, which is the cushion under the pump price.

Trump: no Iran strike before November 3. He said the U.S. is having “productive discussions” with Iran, and some analysts think the comments were meant to lower prices. A deal to reopen the Strait would be the biggest downside risk in this report.

China plans to resume fuel exports. Beijing is expected to restart shipments after its Golden Week holiday, which would ease the pull on the Pacific barrels California competes for. Winter-blend gasoline, authorized September 28, is still in effect.
Our read: We owe you the accounting first. We called a third straight weekly decline in California diesel. AAA came in at $8.3910, up 1.8 cents, 2.1 cents above the top of our range. We were right about wholesale, which fell another 16.6 cents, and right that the rebound of more than 20 cents would not come. We said a third distillate draw would change our mind; it was 0.1 million barrels and we are not counting it. On gasoline we missed 2.3 cents low. Here is why we are changing the call: retail ignored two big wholesale drops in a row. Either the lag is longer than a week or two, or someone is keeping the margin. Add a record-low seasonal diesel stock, a hurricane over two Gulf refineries and Hormuz explosions, and we are not willing to call a third decline into that. We now lean flat to higher, with the record in range. What we’d do with that: keep buying diesel week to week, but this week cover the loads and irrigation sets you can name for the next 10 to 14 days rather than waiting for a drop, and do not sign a term price into a record. On gasoline, buy what you need; winter blend is working, but Los Angeles spot is rising under it. What would prove the diesel call wrong: a Los Angeles spot print for the week ending October 9 down another 15 cents or more, Isaias damage proving minor with Gulf refiners back quickly, or an Iranian yes on Hormuz. GasBuddy’s Patrick De Haan expects only a small effect from the storm. Enverus’s Carl Larry warned that a prolonged Pascagoula shutdown could take weeks to recover from and move prices sharply. We are watching Thursday’s distillate line and that same day’s Los Angeles spot number, not the storm headline.

Section 4

News Behind the Numbers

Global · Highest Impact

Isaias Shut In About 70% of Gulf Oil and Put Two Refineries in Its Path

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida late Friday as a Category 2 storm. Operators evacuated more than 120 platforms and shut in roughly 70% of U.S. Gulf oil output, about 1.3 to 1.5 million barrels a day, with close to 60% of Gulf natural gas also offline. About 500,000 barrels a day of refining capacity is in or near the track, mostly Chevron’s 356,000-barrel Pascagoula, Mississippi plant, which Chevron said was still operating Thursday. WTI settled at $91.85 and Brent at $104.72. What it means for your fuel bill: analysts are split. GasBuddy expects output back within a couple of days and a small price effect. Enverus warned that a refinery shutdown could take weeks to recover from. California’s pump price is set by Pacific Basin barrels, not Gulf Coast ones, but an outage tightens the same global diesel pool, and U.S. diesel stocks are already at a record seasonal low. We will know more once the damage reports are in Monday.

Refining · Stocks

Diesel Stocks Sit at a Record Seasonal Low, and EIA Raised Its Crude Forecast

EIA’s weekly table for the week ending October 2 put distillate inventories at 105.1 million barrels, down 0.1 million, a third straight draw but the smallest of the three. Gasoline stocks rose 0.3 million barrels to 204.7 million. Analyst Andrew Lipow says U.S. diesel stocks are the lowest for this time of year since EIA began collecting data in 1982. EIA’s October outlook raised its fourth-quarter Brent forecast by $14 to $105, expects East Coast distillate stocks to stay about 20% below the five-year average through the coming winter, and sees U.S. retail diesel averaging about $4.50 next year, down from $6.29 in September. The honest read: we told you a third draw would change our mind, and at 100,000 barrels it does not. The level is the story, not the weekly change. The 2027 forecast is a forecast; budget this winter off today’s price.

Geopolitics · Hormuz

Trump Says No Strike on Iran Before November 3, and the Strait Stays Dangerous

Thursday night Trump posted that the U.S. will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms and that the two sides are having “productive discussions.” Some analysts think the comments were meant to lower prices. Iran’s foreign minister said Tehran is reviewing the U.S. response to its seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and will reply within “the next few days.” Friday, Iranian media reported “massive explosions” in the southern Strait and said tankers on unauthorized routes may have struck sea mines. Earlier in the week Iran’s president called the talks “futile” while the vice president said Iran must make a “meaningful” cut to its enrichment capacity. Why it matters on the ground: about 17% of California’s internationally sourced crude in 2025 passed through the Strait, so this is not an abstraction for our refiners. A reopening deal is the biggest downside risk to this report and a mine strike is the biggest upside.

Pacific Supply · China

China Plans to Resume Fuel Exports After Golden Week

Last week China ordered refiners to suspend fuel exports. This week reports say Beijing plans to resume shipments after its Golden Week holiday, which eased some supply concerns Friday. For you: China’s barrels compete with California’s for the same Pacific demand, so a restart would take some pressure off diesel and jet. We would treat it as a plan until cargoes actually show up, because diesel and jet fuel markets stay vulnerable either way.

California · Record Watch

Diesel Is 4.8 Cents Under Its Record, and the Valley Split Five Up, Four Down

California diesel set its all-time record of $8.4387 on September 22 and sits at $8.3910. Regular gasoline eased to $6.3069, against a record of $6.4375 from June 14, 2022. The structure behind both has not changed: the 48.2¢ state excise, cap-and-invest, LCFS costs, a fuel spec nobody else makes, and refining capacity that is shrinking as Phillips 66 and Valero facilities close. In the Valley, Hanford took back 6 cents, Visalia gave back about 6, Bakersfield became the cheapest fill at $8.282 and Madera stayed the dearest at $8.474. What the week did to a load: a 400-mile round trip at 6 mpg burns about 67 gallons. At Fresno’s $8.458 that is $567, about a dollar over last week. Fill that same load in Madera instead of Bakersfield and it costs you about $13 more.

Section 5

What To Do This Week

✓ Action Items

1. Keep buying diesel week to week, and cover the next 10 to 14 days. Wholesale is down 49.5 cents in two prints but the pump is 4.8 cents from a record and Gulf refiners are in a hurricane’s path. Cover the loads and irrigation sets you can name now, and do not sign a term price into a record.
2. Ask where the wholesale drop went. Los Angeles diesel spot fell 16.6 cents more and retail rose 1.8. Get the rack basis on every delivery ticket in writing, with language that moves your price down when the rack moves down, and ask your supplier about any ticket that has not.
3. Buy gasoline as you need it. Regular fell 8.2 cents and winter blend is working, but Los Angeles gasoline spot rose 17.1 and then 14.4 cents in the last two prints. Do not run the shop fleet dry waiting for lower.
4. Book propane now. Mont Belvieu is back above a dollar, up about 10 cents in a week and 17% on the month. Finish your frost-protection, dryer and heater gallons before November.
5. Index your surcharges to California. The DOE national average fell 18.3 cents while California fell 9.9, and the California premium widened to $1.88. Put the EIA California series or a West Coast rack in writing on every hauling contract.
