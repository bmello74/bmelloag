📈  This Week’s Call

Diesel took out its record at $8.5123, and crude’s 10% jump says the pump has more to go. We called flat to higher, and the call held.

Last Monday we called California diesel flat to higher and put the odds of a new record near even. The record fell. AAA’s Sunday read puts diesel at $8.5123, up 12.1 cents on the week and 7.4 cents above the $8.4387 set September 22. Our range was $8.34 to $8.47, so the actual landed 4.2 cents over the top: right direction, too timid on size. EIA’s survey for the week of October 12 printed $8.091, up 0.9 cents, inside our $8.05 to $8.20 range, against a national average of $6.179, down 2.0 cents. Gasoline went the way we said, by more than we allowed. Regular finished at $6.1782, down 12.9 cents and 25.9 cents under its $6.4375 record, 4.2 cents below the bottom of our $6.22 to $6.37 range; EIA regular printed $6.104, inside our $6.10 to $6.22 range. Wholesale turned. Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel spot rose 14.1 cents to $5.011 for the week ending October 9, after falling 49.5 cents over the two prints before it, while Los Angeles gasoline spot fell 55.4 cents to $3.884. The Gulf storm we flagged turned out minor, but crude did not care. Crude jumped. WTI settled Friday at $101.41, up 10.4% on the week and above our $85 to $99 range, and Brent at $109.86, up 4.9% and its highest settlement since May 19, as Iranian attacks on shipping cut Hormuz flows. Our read: a $9.56 move in crude is worth about 23 cents a gallon at the pump over the next one to two weeks, US diesel stocks are down to 102.6 million barrels, and wholesale diesel has stopped falling. We lean higher on diesel, with another record likely, and flat to lower on gasoline.

Well & Pump Efficiency

Diesel is at a record, and harvest is winding down. That is the week to find out which pumps are bleeding you. The well is about to go quiet, and bowl work, impeller trimming and machine work cost the least in the off-season when nobody is waiting on water. At $8.54 diesel the cost of a worn plant has never been higher. A worn diesel-driven pump costs about 1.8 times what the identical wear costs on the meter. Here is what that is worth in dollars.

Lift Electric @65% Electric @50% Diesel loss Electric loss
200 ft 315 kWh · $110/AF 409 kWh · $143/AF $60/AF · $208/ac $33/AF · $116/ac
400 ft 630 kWh · $220/AF 819 kWh · $287/AF $119/AF · $417/ac $66/AF · $231/ac
700 ft 1,102 kWh · $386/AF 1,433 kWh · $502/AF $208/AF · $729/ac $116/AF · $405/ac

Electric at 35¢/kWh blended. Diesel at Fresno’s $8.542 pump (AAA, 10/18); off-road dyed runs about 72.6¢ lower, near $7.82. Per-acre figures assume 3.5 acre-feet on mature almonds. “Loss” is the extra cost of running a plant at 50% efficiency instead of a healthy 65%. Published guidance says 49% or less means retrofit, repair or adjustment.

What that is on a real block. A 700-foot diesel well serving 40 acres of mature almonds at 50% efficiency is burning about $29,200 a season in pure waste, money that buys you no water and no crop. The same well on the meter wastes $16,200. A 400-foot diesel plant on 40 acres is $16,700. The 1.8-to-1 ratio holds at every depth, and it is the whole argument for testing your diesel units first when you can only afford to test a few.

And here is the part that matters in SGMA country. A rising power bill does not tell you whether the pump is worn or the water simply got deeper. Those are different problems with different fixes, a bowl job versus a deeper bore or a lower pump setting, and a test is the only thing that separates them. Money spent on the wrong one is gone. A tester takes four readings: standing water level and pumping water level (the difference is your drawdown), flow rate and discharge pressure, and power input, kWh draw for electric or fuel rate for diesel. Those four give total dynamic head and therefore overall plant efficiency. Two cheap outcomes are worth knowing about: turbine pumps are often simply out of adjustment, and impeller clearance can sometimes be reset on the spot for immediate savings. A badly worn or corroded impeller cannot be adjusted back, though. That is machine work or replacement.
Who pays for the test. The Advanced Pumping Efficiency Program (APEP) is funded through the Public Purpose Programs charge you already pay on your PG&E bill and administered by the Center for Irrigation Technology at Fresno State. 800.845.6038. The subsidy goes to the testing company: $200 for a pump not tested in the prior 47 months, $100 if not tested in 23 months, $50 for a pump in series with another. You need a non-residential PG&E electric or gas account used for irrigation, 25 nameplate horsepower or more, paying the Public Purpose charge. One subsidized test per pump per 23 months. The subsidy may not cover the whole test, so get the total price in writing before you schedule. APEP also runs a separate diesel pumping efficiency track, which is the one to ask for at these prices. Retrofit cash incentives exist for bowl or impeller replacement, machine work and impeller trimming, but they require a pump test before and after, no more than three years apart. A VFD or motor swap by itself does not qualify.

Also live, and worth moving on: the state’s WETA program funds no-cost irrigation evaluations and pump efficiency tests through technical-assistance providers, and many of those grants expire during 2026. With about ten weeks left in the year, that window is closing on a calendar, not on a market. If you want a free evaluation out of it, call now rather than in January.

News Behind the Numbers

Global · Highest Impact

Crude Jumped 10% as Hormuz Flows Collapsed, and Brent Hit Its Highest Settlement Since May

WTI settled Friday at $101.41, up $9.56 or 10.4% on the week, and Brent at $109.86, up 4.9% and its highest settlement since May 19. Kpler data cited by AFP show oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz fell from about 20.8 million barrels on October 8 to 2.9 million on October 11, and UKMTO counted 23 attacks since the start of October. A missile aimed at Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea port of Yanbu was reportedly intercepted. The IEA has planned a release of more than 20 million barrels and the US has offered up to 17 million, which capped the gains. What it means for your fuel bill: a $10 move in a barrel of crude is worth about 24 cents a gallon, so this week’s $9.56 gain is roughly 23 cents of diesel pressure that retail has not fully shown yet, and it typically arrives in one to two weeks. Iran says shipping could resume within six days if Washington accepts its seven conditions. Washington has not, and Trump reportedly expects a deal only after the November 3 midterms.

Refining · Stocks

Diesel Stocks Fell Again and a Russian Refinery Went Down

EIA’s weekly table for the week ending October 9 put US distillate inventories at 102.6 million barrels, down 2.5 million from 105.1 million, a far bigger draw than the 0.1 million the week before. Commercial crude stocks fell 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million, a larger draw than expected. Ukrainian drones hit Russia’s Ryazan refinery early October 15 and shut one of its main crude units; the plant handles roughly 340,000 barrels a day. November ULSD futures settled at $4.8991 on Friday, up 3.4% on the week. Why it matters on the ground: diesel is the tight product in the world right now, and California competes for the same Pacific barrels. We told you the level of stocks was the story, and this week the level got lower.

California · Wholesale

Los Angeles Diesel Turned Up and Gasoline Spot Collapsed

EIA’s weekly spot series for the week ending October 9 put Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel at $5.011, up 14.1 cents after two straight declines totaling 49.5 cents, and Los Angeles gasoline at $3.884, down 55.4 cents. In a post cited by the Daily Caller on October 14, GasBuddy’s Patrick De Haan said Los Angeles wholesale diesel set a record, up 52 cents, and San Francisco was up 21 cents. We could not confirm that figure, so treat it as a signal and not a print. California Trucking Association president Tim Pollock told Trucking Dive that fuel surcharges cover only about 70% to 75% of a carrier’s costs. For you: the cushion that held retail down for two weeks is gone, and haulers are eating cost they will try to pass to you. Read your surcharge language before the invoice does.

Policy · Relief Talk

Federal Dyed-Diesel Move Does Little in California, and a State Tax Suspension Is Only a Proposal

Trucking Dive reports the Trump administration recently ordered dyed diesel allowed on the road, with fuel taxes deferred for the rest of the year. The California Farm Bureau notes California restricts tax-free fuel on public roads, so it does little here, and the Farm Bureau has floated suspending the state diesel tax. That is a proposal, not law. Truckers have also asked Governor Newsom for help. What it means for your costs: nothing changes at the rack this week. Do not put dyed fuel in a road vehicle in California on the strength of a federal headline; the state rules have not moved and the penalties are real. Budget off the price on the sign.

What To Do This Week

✓ Action Items

1. Keep buying diesel week to week, and cover the next 10 to 14 days. Crude is up $9.56 a barrel, wholesale has stopped falling and the pump is at a record. Cover the loads and irrigation sets you can name now, and do not sign a term price into a record.
2. Get the rack basis in writing. Wholesale and retail are now moving the same direction, which is when a cost-plus contract either works for you or does not. Ask for the rack reference on every delivery ticket, with language that moves your price down when the rack moves down.
3. Buy gasoline as you need it. Regular fell 12.9 cents and Los Angeles gasoline spot fell 55.4 cents, so the pump should keep easing early in the week. Do not tank up early; crude’s jump limits how far it goes.
4. Book propane now. Mont Belvieu is about $1.10, up 30% on the month and 72% on the year. Finish your frost-protection, dryer and heater gallons before November.
5. Index your surcharges to California. The DOE national average fell 2.0 cents while California rose 0.9, and the California premium widened to $1.91. Put the EIA California series or a West Coast rack in writing on every hauling contract.
6. Test your diesel pumps first. A worn diesel plant wastes about 1.8 times what the same wear costs on the meter, up to $729 an acre at 700 feet. Call APEP at 800.845.6038 and get the total test price in writing.
