📈  This Week’s Call

Diesel slipped 5.7 cents and stayed under its record, but wholesale jumped 31 cents and Brent hit a six-month high. We called it higher, and the pump did not follow yet.

Last Monday we called California diesel higher at $8.50 to $8.72 and put the odds of another record near four in five. That call missed. AAA’s Sunday read puts diesel at $8.4550, down 5.7 cents on the week and 5.7 cents under the $8.5123 record set October 18, 4.5 cents below the bottom of our range. We had the direction wrong and the record odds too high. EIA’s survey for the week of October 19 printed $8.242, up 15.1 cents, inside our $8.15 to $8.30 range, against a national average of $6.276, up 9.7 cents. Gasoline went the way we said. Regular finished at $6.0236, down 15.5 cents and inside our $6.02 to $6.20 range, right at the bottom; EIA regular printed $6.031, inside our $5.97 to $6.10 range. Wholesale turned up hard. Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel spot averaged $5.324 for the week ending October 16, up 31.3 cents, while Los Angeles gasoline spot fell 24.9 cents to $3.635. Crude climbed. WTI settled Friday at $103.27, up 2.3% on the week per Reuters and inside our $94 to $108 range, and Brent at $116.72, up about 6% and its highest settlement since April 29, as Houthi attacks hit Saudi Arabia. Our read: the pump has not passed the wholesale move through. Los Angeles diesel is up 35.4 cents since the week ending October 9 while the statewide pump is up only 6.4 cents, and Brent just made a new six-month high. A distillate stock build is the one thing pulling the other way. We lean higher on diesel, with a smaller call than last week, and lower on gasoline.

Last Week at the Pump

AAA’s Sunday, October 25 read puts California diesel at $8.4550, down 5.7 cents on the week and 5.7 cents under the October 18 record of $8.5123. Regular finished at $6.0236, down 15.5 cents. EIA samples Monday and AAA reports Sunday, so the two will not always agree on direction in a given week. Here is the number that matters: eight of nine Valley metros slipped, and only Merced rose. The statewide price is still within 6 cents of its record, and wholesale is now moving the other way.

Diesel detail: Off-road dyed diesel is exempt from California’s 48.2¢ state excise and the 24.4¢ federal excise, roughly a 72.6¢ per gallon spread before sales tax. At Fresno’s $8.533 pump that puts dyed product near $7.81 a gallon, about $7,807 to fill a 1,000-gallon on-farm tank, roughly $9 less than last week. Price your rack and cardlock against these numbers, not the sign on the highway. Here is the test for your delivered price: Los Angeles diesel spot is up 35.4 cents since the week ending October 9 ($5.011 to the $5.365 daily print on October 20), while the statewide pump is up only 6.4 cents over the same two weeks ($8.3910 to $8.4550). Wholesale leads the pump by one to two weeks, so some of that gap is timing, and it says your price is more likely to rise than fall this week. If your delivered price has already risen by more than the wholesale move explains, ask your supplier where the difference went.

For your surcharge tables. The DOE national on-highway diesel average of $6.276 for the week of October 19 governs fuel surcharges this week. It rose 9.7 cents while California rose 15.1, so the California premium over the national number widened to $1.97 a gallon from $1.91. The fix has not changed: index to the EIA California series or a West Coast rack, get it in writing, and stop letting a Gulf Coast number price a Central Valley load.

▲ Pushing Prices Up

Crude made a six-month high. Brent settled at $116.72, up $3.55 on the day and about 6% on the week. Houthi attacks hit targets across Saudi Arabia, including energy infrastructure; Saudi Aramco ordered its Dhahran headquarters evacuated and the State Department ordered non-essential personnel out. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claims seven tankers were hit. UKMTO confirmed two attacks on tankers this week; we cannot confirm the rest.

Wholesale diesel is running ahead of the pump. Los Angeles diesel spot averaged $5.324 for the week ending October 16, up 31.3 cents, and the daily prints on October 19 and 20 held at $5.383 and $5.365.

The cushion is thin. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is down to 282.9 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982, and the American Trucking Associations’ chief economist says he does not expect much relief.

▼ Holding Prices Down

Distillate stocks built. EIA put US distillate inventories at 104.3 million barrels for the week ending October 16, up 1.68 million when analysts expected a 1.16 million draw. Refinery utilization held at 93.4%.

Gasoline wholesale keeps falling. Los Angeles gasoline spot fell 24.9 cents to $3.635, and winter-blend gasoline is in effect early under Governor Newsom’s September 28 order. Retail fell 15.5 cents and has room to fall further.

Some of this is risk premium, not lost barrels. Analyst Andrew Lipow notes flows out of Hormuz have increased, so the price gain reflects the risk of disruption. A quiet weekend can take some of it back. We are not planning around a deal this week.
Our read: The scorecard first. We called California diesel higher at $8.50 to $8.72 and said another record was four in five. AAA came in at $8.4550, 4.5 cents under the bottom of our range and 5.7 cents under the record. We missed on direction and we were too sure of ourselves. EIA diesel at $8.242 landed inside our range. On gasoline we called lower and got it: AAA regular at $6.0236 sat at the bottom of our range and EIA at $6.031 inside ours. On crude WTI settled at $103.27, inside our $94 to $108, but the Brent–WTI spread came in at $13.45, over the top of our $7 to $13. We named three things that would prove the diesel call wrong. A Los Angeles spot print down another 15 cents did not come; it rose 31.3. WTI under $92 by Thursday did not come. A Hormuz acceptance did not come. The call was wrong anyway, and the reason is retail: the pump lagged the wholesale move. Here is why we still lean higher, with a smaller call: wholesale is up 35.4 cents in two weeks, the pump only 6.4, and Brent is at a six-month high. That gap does not usually stay open. The offset is a 1.68 million barrel distillate build. What we’d do with that: keep buying diesel week to week, but cover the loads and sets you can name for the next 10 to 14 days now rather than waiting for a drop, and do not sign a term price near a record. On gasoline, buy what you need; it is drifting lower. What would prove the diesel call wrong: a Los Angeles spot print for the week ending October 23 down 15 cents or more, Brent back under $108 by Thursday, or Washington and Tehran announcing a Hormuz deal. We are watching Wednesday’s distillate line and that day’s Los Angeles spot number.

Section 4

News Behind the Numbers

Global · Highest Impact

Brent Closed at $116.72, a Six-Month High, as Houthi Attacks Hit Saudi Arabia

Brent settled Friday at $116.72, up $3.55 on the day, about 6% on the week and its highest settlement since April 29. WTI settled at $103.27, up 2.3% on the week per Reuters (the front month rolled to December on October 20). Houthi attacks hit targets across Saudi Arabia, including energy infrastructure, Aramco ordered its Dhahran headquarters evacuated and the State Department ordered non-essential personnel out. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claims seven tankers were hit; UKMTO confirmed two attacks on tankers this week and the rest are unconfirmed. Traders increasingly expect US strikes on Iran to resume within weeks, and President Trump said Washington is not negotiating with Tehran. What it means for your fuel bill: a $10 move in a barrel of crude is worth about 24 cents a gallon, so Brent’s $6.86 gain this week is roughly 16 cents of diesel pressure that the pump has not fully shown, and it typically arrives in one to two weeks. Analysts at FinanceFeeds put the base case for Brent at $112 to $120 next week.

Refining · Stocks

Diesel Stocks Built When Analysts Expected a Draw, but the Reserve Is Nearly Empty

EIA’s weekly table for the week ending October 16 put US distillate inventories at 104.3 million barrels, up 1.68 million when analysts expected a 1.16 million draw. Commercial crude stocks fell 3.06 million barrels to 412.5 million, gasoline stocks fell 2.95 million to 205.3 million, and refinery utilization held at 93.4%. Separately, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen to 282.9 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982, and the American Trucking Associations’ chief economist says distillate stocks “do not bode well” for fall freight prices. Why it matters on the ground: the build is the one bearish number in a bullish week, and it is why our call is smaller than last week’s. It does not fix a reserve running out of barrels to sell.

California · Wholesale

Los Angeles Diesel Jumped 31 Cents While the Pump Slipped

EIA’s weekly spot series for the week ending October 16 put Los Angeles ultra-low-sulfur diesel at $5.324, up 31.3 cents after a 14.1 cent rise the week before, and Los Angeles gasoline at $3.635, down 24.9 cents and down 80.3 cents in two weeks. Daily diesel prints held at $5.383 and $5.365 on October 19 and 20. Meanwhile AAA’s statewide diesel slipped 5.7 cents to $8.4550. Governor Newsom’s September 28 order moved winter-blend gasoline up to immediate sale, which Hoodline reports costs refiners about 15 cents a gallon less to make; AAA’s Doug Johnson says October is still poised to be the most expensive October on record for gas. For you: the gap between wholesale and pump is the most useful number on this page, and it points up for diesel and down for gasoline.

Policy · Relief

USDA Announces an $8 Billion Harvest Fuel Relief Program, With No Details Yet

President Trump announced Monday, October 19 that USDA will launch an $8 billion harvest fuel relief program administered by the Farm Service Agency, with one-time payments under a uniform formula meant to cover part of modeled 2026 losses. The reports we found did not give eligibility, a sign-up date, per-acre rates or any mention of California. What it means for your costs: nothing changes at the rack this week, and we would not budget against money that has no rules yet. Keep your fuel receipts and acreage records current, and watch for an FSA notice. California’s state tax rules on dyed fuel have not moved, and dyed fuel does not belong in a road vehicle here.

Harvest · Season

Almond Harvest Ran Early, Which Shifts Where the Diesel Gets Burned

The California almond harvest started about 10 days earlier than typical, with San Joaquin Valley orchards a few days to a week ahead, and processors will keep receiving almonds into November with processing expected to finish earlier than usual, per FreshPlaza on October 13. Why it matters on the ground: for almond growers more of the shaking, sweeping and hauling diesel is likely already burned, so the exposure now moves to nut delivery, fall field work and frost-protection fuel. Propane is the one to price now.

What To Do This Week

✓ Action Items

1. Keep buying diesel week to week, and cover the next 10 to 14 days. Los Angeles wholesale is up 35 cents in two weeks, the pump only 6, and Brent is at a six-month high. Cover the loads and irrigation sets you can name now, and do not sign a term price near a record.
2. Get the rack basis in writing. Wholesale and retail are moving different directions, which is when a cost-plus contract either works for you or does not. Ask for the rack reference on every delivery ticket, with language that moves your price down when the rack moves down.
3. Buy gasoline as you need it. Regular fell 15.5 cents and Los Angeles gasoline spot has fallen 80 cents in two weeks, so the pump should keep easing. Do not tank up early.
4. Book propane this week. Mont Belvieu is about $1.17, up 32% on the month and 79% on the year. Finish your frost-protection, dryer and heater gallons before November.
5. Index your surcharges to California. The DOE national average rose 9.7 cents while California rose 15.1, and the California premium widened to $1.97. Put the EIA California series or a West Coast rack in writing on every hauling contract.
6. Watch for the USDA fuel relief notice. The $8 billion harvest fuel program has no published rules yet and California was not mentioned. Keep fuel receipts and acreage records ready, and do not count on it in your budget.
