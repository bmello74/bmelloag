Shaklee has been developing nutrition products since 1956. What caught my attention about the company is that it has spent decades researching nutrition, with 110+ published clinical studies and 70+ patents and patents pending. Its quality standards go well beyond the finished label, including screening new botanical ingredients for more than 350 contaminants and conducting more than 100,000 quality tests each year.

But the reason Michelle is the one I recommend is much simpler.

She lives it.

She takes care of herself, stays active, pays attention to what she puts in her body, and uses the same products she shares with other people.

Michelle believes wellness should be personal and practical. There isn’t one routine that makes sense for everyone. Where you are in life, what you’re trying to improve, and what your everyday life actually looks like all matter.

So she starts with a conversation.

Where are you now?
What are you hoping to improve?
What are you already doing?
And what would actually be realistic for you?

Then she helps you find a place to start.

Sometimes that includes Shaklee products. Sometimes it’s a simple change. And if she doesn’t think you need something, she’ll tell you.

That matters to me.

Shaklee’s Landmark Studies, conducted in collaboration with researchers at UC Berkeley, looked at long-term supplement users and found higher blood nutrient levels and healthier measures across several biomarkers compared with non-users. A 10-year follow-up continued to examine those long-term users.
